Welcome!

Meet Cath

Naturopathic Herbalist

When you work with me, you’re working with someone who has spent over 25 years immersed in both the art and science of herbal medicine.

My clinical foundation was shaped by the late Dorothy Hall, one of Australia’s most respected traditional herbalists. From her, I learned that herbal medicine is not simply about plants. I continue to undertake advanced clinical education and mentoring, including specialist women's health mentoring in peri and menopause and advanced microbiome and gastrointestinal training with Dr Jason Hawrelak.

Perimenopause is the 5-8 years leading up to menopause (12 months after your last menstrual period). It is a time of hormonal transition that can greatly impact your mood and nervous system. Naturopath and author Lara Briden has compared perimenopause to second puberty! And the graph below nicely illustrates the hormonal fluctuations of perimenopause...

Symptoms during this time can include:

Mood changes

Menstrual changes and irregularities

Sleep disruption

Headaches and migraines

Energy shifts

Weight gain

How I can help

Natural therapies: herbal tonics, targeted nutrients, and practical lifestyle adjustments can genuinely support you through this transition, easing symptoms while supporting your long-term wellbeing rather than just masking what’s happening.

I can specifically help with:

sleep and nervous system changes

mood, anxiety and emotional resilience

changing cycles and premenstrual symptoms

fatigue, weight and metabolic changes

digestive and microbiome health

bone, cardiovascular and healthy ageing foundations

working collaboratively with a GP when MHT or further medical investigation may be appropriate

Women's health in midlife has become a central focus of my practice. I undertake ongoing specialist mentoring in perimenopause and menopause with experienced clinicians alongside continuing education in herbal medicine, nutrition, healthy ageing and microbiome health.

Breast cancer

I have a particular interest and depth of experience in supporting women through breast cancer from diagnosis, through treatment, and into recovery. This includes:

•          Supporting nutrition, resilience and recovery during treatment (with all recommendations considered alongside your oncology care)

•          Managing treatment side effects naturopathically, safely alongside your oncology care

•          Rebuilding strength, energy and resilience once active treatment ends

•          Longer-term support for recovery, resilience and overall health and vitality

Physical symptoms such as post-cancer fatigue are very common following treatment. And many women feel the emotional impact of a cancer diagnosis and treatment linger after completing their treatment.

Individualised herbal tonics are very helpful, in combination with lifestyle tweaks and focussed nutritional support.

My background in this area started in 2000 when I studied cancer support strategies with Herbalist, Nancy Evelyn while practicing in Newcastle. Then in 2018, I completed further integrative oncology study with American Naturopath Lise Alschuler. Both these practitioners helped provide me a solid context of understanding cancer and how best to support people who have had a cancer diagnosis.

In 2021, I completed a research degree through the University of Tasmania and my final piece of research was focussed on a topic of my own choosing. I chose to investigate medicinal mushrooms as potential adjunct treatment for breast cancer patients following oncology treatments. This research was published in the Journal of the Naturopaths and Herbalists Association of Australia in 2021 and was a finalist for article of the year.

A breast cancer diagnosis changes everything, often in an instant. One day life looks one way; the next, you're absorbing news that reorders your priorities, your calendar, and your sense of safety in your own body; all while somehow needing to make fast decisions about surgery and possible treatment.

Support matters at every stage of this journey, not just at the end of it. From the shock of diagnosis, through surgery and oncology treatment, into recovery, and finally into life beyond treatment, each phase asks something different of you, and each deserves its own kind of care.

The shock of a new diagnosis

The period right after diagnosis is often one of the hardest, and it rarely gets talked about enough. In a short space of time, you're absorbing frightening news, meeting a stream of new specialists, and being asked to make significant decisions about surgery and treatment, often while still in shock.

Common experiences in this phase include:

Difficulty thinking clearly or making decisions

Disrupted sleep and a racing mind

Overwhelming anxiety about what's ahead

A sense that everything is happening too fast

This is a time when nervous system support matters enormously, not to erase what you're going through, but to help steady you enough to think clearly, sleep a little better, and get through appointments and decisions with a bit more resilience.

Getting through surgery and oncology treatment

Surgery, chemotherapy, radiation; whatever combination your treatment involves, this phase asks a huge amount of your body and your reserves. It's physically demanding and emotionally relentless, often over many months.

During this time, support can help with:

Preparing the body for surgery and supporting recovery afterwards

Managing treatment side effects such as nausea, fatigue, and skin sensitivity

Supporting immune resilience through treatment

Steadying anxiety and sleep through an intense and uncertain period

The aim isn't to replace anything your oncology team is doing; it's to work alongside it, supporting your body's own resilience so you're better equipped to get through treatment.

The gap after treatment ends

Oncology teams do extraordinary work getting women through diagnosis and treatment. But once the active treatment phase is over, many women find themselves without a clear map for what comes next: physically, emotionally, or practically.

Ongoing fatigue that doesn't resolve with rest

Brain fog or difficulty concentrating

Disrupted sleep

Anxiety about recurrence

A body that feels unfamiliar, even after physical recovery

Wanting to move forward, but not quite knowing how

None of this means anything has gone wrong. It means your body and nervous system are still recalibrating after a major event and that recalibration deserves just as much attention as the treatment itself did.

Supporting the body's own capacity to recover

This is where a whole-person, naturopathic approach can add real value alongside the oncology care you've already received. The focus isn't on replacing anything your medical team has done; it's on supporting your body's own resilience in the months and years that follow.

In practice, this can look like:

Rebuilding energy.  Targeted nutritional support and individualised herbal tonics to help address post-treatment fatigue, which is one of the most common and most under-treated after-effects of cancer treatment.

Supporting immune resilience.  Many women want extra support for their immune system during and after treatment. There is growing research interest in medicinal mushrooms, such as Reishi, as a potential adjunct in this space.

Steadying the nervous system.  Anxiety after a cancer diagnosis is common and completely understandable. Nervous system support through herbal medicine, nutrition, and lifestyle changes can make a meaningful difference to day-to-day quality of life.

Restoring a sense of agency.  For many women, having a proactive, structured plan for their own recovery, something they're actively doing, not just waiting through, is itself part of feeling like themselves again.

Thriving looks different at every stage

There's no single timeline for "getting back to normal," and for many women, what comes after treatment isn't a return to how things were before. It's a new chapter, built with a bit more awareness of what matters and what their body needs.

Thriving might mean:

Having steady energy to be present for the people and things you care about

Feeling less anxious about your body and your health

Understanding what your body needs and being equipped to support it

Simply feeling like yourself again, even if "yourself" looks a little different now

You don't have to navigate this alone

Whether you've just received a diagnosis, are in the middle of surgery or oncology treatment, or are further along in your recovery, support is available at every stage and you don't need to wait until treatment ends to reach out.

Together we can build a plan that meets you where you are: supporting your nervous system through the shock of diagnosis, your resilience through treatment, and your energy and sense of self as you move toward thriving beyond it.

Reach out to book a consultation and talk about what support could look like for you, wherever you are on this journey.

There’s a stage of life that doesn’t get talked about enough. It’s the stage where your hormones are shifting, your body might be asking new things of you, and life is somehow busier than ever. I call it midlife and beyond, and after 25 years in clinical practice, it’s become the heart of my work.

I want to explain why, and what that means for how I support the women who come to see me.

It started with noticing a pattern

Over the years, I began to notice that so many of the women sitting across from me were dealing with some version of the same story. A woman in her late 40s whose periods had become unpredictable and whose sleep had fallen apart. A woman recovering from breast cancer treatment, wanting to feel strong in her body again. A woman holding down a career, raising teenagers and caring for ageing parents, running on empty with no idea how to refill the tank.

Different presentations, but a common thread: this is a life stage that asks a great deal of women, often while their bodies are also changing beneath them. And it’s a stage that deserves proper, dedicated care.

The three places I focus my work

Perimenopause and beyond.  The hormonal transition into menopause can affect far more than your cycle: mood, sleep, energy and your nervous system all shift too. I’ve become increasingly interested in this space, including presenting on perimenopause at a community health night in 2023, and I continue to see how much relief is possible with the right herbal and nutritional support.

Midlife health challenges.  This is the stage of life where new diagnoses have a way of surfacing; autoimmune conditions, osteopenia, thyroid changes, and for some, breast cancer. I have a particular interest in supporting women through breast cancer specifically, working alongside medical treatment to support the body’s resilience before, during and after.

Stress and resilience.  So many women in this stage are holding more than they let on. Chronic stress doesn’t just affect mood, over time it affects hormones, immunity and long-term health. Building resilience here isn’t about adding one more task to an already full plate; it’s about steady, sustainable support.

What ties it all together

Whatever brings a woman to see me, my approach is the same. I look at the whole picture, not just your symptoms, but your life stage, your nervous system, and what’s being asked of you day to day. I believe in strong foundations rather than quick fixes: nourishment, sleep, emotional balance and connection, supported by carefully chosen herbal medicine and nutritional tweaks.

The body has an innate intelligence. My role is to help create the conditions for it to respond;

with care, clarity and depth, at exactly the stage of life when you need it most.

If you’re navigating perimenopause, a new diagnosis, or simply feel like you’re running on empty, I’d be honoured to support you.
