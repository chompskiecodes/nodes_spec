## LOW LEVEL LASER THERAPY
### About Laser Therapy
Low level laser therapy (LLLT), otherwise known as photobiomodulation, is a non-invasive treatment which uses red and near-infrared light to reduce pain and inflammation and works to accelerate tissue repair and regeneration. It harnesses the power of light to provide pain relief which enhances your body's ability to heal. LLLT can be used to treat many conditions such as plantar fasciitis (heel pain), tendonitis, arthritis and neurological issues. It is also used to help with post-operative healing and regeneration.
### How Laser Therapy Works
This treatment works by simulating a photochemical effect which promotes connective tissue cells as well as improving lymphatic drainage to assist with swelling. The laser also dilates the blood vessels in the area of application which works to increase blood flow and tissue repair. Additionally, the body's nerve signals are regulated by the laser and this reduces pain levels. When combined with the rest of your treatment plan, LLLT gives you the best chance to return to your normal activities safely and injury-free.
### Safety
LLLT is non-invasive and safe. No harmful side effects associated with LLLT have been reported and the treatment is safe for all ages.
### Conditions Laser Therapy Can Treat
Laser therapy is able to treat ankle sprain, rheumatoid arthritis, osteo arthritis, lower back pain, leg swelling, plantar fasciitis, peroneal tendonitis, achilles tendonitis, post operative pain, nerve pain, hip bursitis and Morton's neuroma.
### Treatment Dosage
The recommended treatment dose is twice a week for 3-6 weeks, however this also depends on the severity of the condition.

## SHOCKWAVE THERAPY
### About Shockwave Therapy
Shockwave therapy is a non-invasive treatment. It works by stimulating the growth of new blood vessels in the tissue which promotes growth factors, increases cell proliferation, decreases inflammation and mobilises stem cells. Shockwave utilises acoustic energy which builds up in the body's cells. The handpiece is applied with a constant pressure to the affected body part. This concentrated effect ensures the body's cells exert an anti-inflammatory effect which induces healing and reduces pain.
### How Shockwave Therapy Helps
Shockwave therapy provides a mechanical stimulus to the targeted tissue and surrounding area. This stimulus kick starts the tissue healing process within the injured tendon, muscles and bone which enhances tissue regeneration and remodeling. Clinical evidence proves shockwave therapy significantly reduces pain due to its analgesic effect. The mechanically induced stimulus increases blood flow and growth factors to the injured sites, which helps in reducing inflammation in the tendons, muscles and bone.
### Conditions Shockwave Therapy Treats
Its use in the lower limb primarily treats musculoskeletal conditions such as plantar fasciitis, achilles tendinopathy and patella tendinopathy. It has recently been adapted for osteoarthritis of the knee and big toe joint. Shockwave therapy also treats ankle sprains, muscle strain, trigger points and plantar fibroma.
### Treatment Dosage
The treatment dosage depends on the severity of the condition. A course of shockwave treatment is usually once a week for 3 consecutive weeks.

## ORTHOTIC THERAPY
### About Orthotics
Orthotics are a special shoe insert used to treat numerous foot conditions. Orthotics alter the biomechanics of your gait and how you run by redistributing the ground reaction forces (the pressure caused by the impact of your foot on the ground). They reduce foot pain and allow us to prevent excessive forces which may lead to injury. There are many different types and materials of orthotics which can be customised to suit you. Due to the flexibility and range of options, orthotics can be used to treat numerous foot conditions. They can even be designed to fit into high heels.
### Custom 3D-Printed Orthotics
At Bounce Podiatry, our custom orthoses are 3D-printed. Made to measure for your feet, they are precisely cut which results in an excellent fit. Custom foot orthoses are recommended for those who require a significant degree of control, are hyperirritable to generic orthoses, or would like them fitted into special footwear such as footy boots.
### How the Orthotic Process Works
The process of making custom foot orthoses starts with a thorough consultation and biomechanical examination with your podiatrist. This allows us to understand your lifestyle, activities and goals. We assess how your entire body functions, what footwear you have and what pathologies or issues you have that can be managed with the orthoses.
If the podiatrist deems having foot orthoses is beneficial in your treatment plan, the next step would be to take a 3D laser scan of the foot. A special laser scanner is placed onto a tablet which enables us to take a 3D image of the foot. This process takes less than 5 minutes. It is safe, non-invasive and much less messy than taking a plaster cast.
Next, the podiatrist works alongside the orthotic laboratory to create your prescription using a computer-assisted design program. The prescription depends on what type of footwear the orthoses will be going into, the shape and biomechanics of the foot, and what the podiatrist wants to achieve. There are hundreds of ways a podiatrist can modify an orthotic prescription to suit each patient.
After previewing the final design, the orthoses are manufactured by a 3D printer which layers the printing material based on the preview model. Once printed, the foot orthoses are hot so they are left to cool for up to 24 hours. The process of making a pair of foot orthoses from a scan takes approximately 3 days, then they are ready to be sent to the clinic.

## PACT MED FUNGAL NAIL THERAPY
### About Fungal Nail Infection
Fungal nail infection, or onychomycosis, is a condition where a fungus infiltrates the nail bed and plate. This causes brittle, crumbly and discoloured nails. Often, the main problem is cosmetic in nature due to the change in appearance. A fungal infection is often stubborn to treat. Oral drugs are the most effective but can potentially cause severe side effects. Topical treatments carry less side effects, however they are much less effective and can take up to 12 months of continuous application to see results.
### How PACT med Therapy Works
PACT, or photodynamic antimicrobial therapy, is a treatment that uses a combination of a photosensitizer and a light source. It is a technology that uses light to eliminate fungi, bacteria and viruses. The photosensitizer gel is applied to the affected nails and exposed to a light. The light interacts with the gel to produce a photochemical reaction which targets infected tissue in and around the nail. This effectively kills the fungus, leaving your nails looking good and free of infection.
### Treatment Course
The recommended treatment is 3 consecutive sessions within 1 week, followed by a review 4 weeks later. During this time, we recommend you file the nail and apply our topical antifungal compound mixture. This ensures any fungal spores are targeted and killed which delivers the best results. PACT Fungal Laser Therapy is effective and non-invasive. No systemic side-effects have been reported. Pactmed fungal laser therapy can improve the appearance of fungal nails in 80% of cases.

### About Diagnostic Ultrasound
Bounce Podiatry is the only podiatrist in Western Australia to offer a diagnostic foot ultrasound as part of your routine appointment. Diagnostic ultrasound is a safe and non-invasive medical imaging modality used to dynamically assess different structures such as muscle, tendon, ligament and bone. Images and video can be taken live to assess how different tissues are behaving. There is minimal to no pain or discomfort when having an ultrasound examination.
### Why Ultrasound at Bounce Podiatry
Bounce podiatrists are experienced in using and interpreting diagnostic ultrasound. This saves you time as there is no need to arrange a separate ultrasound. It also means you receive immediate feedback on your injury. We use ultrasound to review your progress and to track any changes during your treatment journey.
### Conditions Assessed With Ultrasound
We commonly use the diagnostic ultrasound to assess many different conditions including plantar fasciitis and plantar fascia tears, Achilles tendinopathy, heel spurring, Morton's neuroma and intermetatarsal neuritis, arthritis in the midfoot or big toe joint, bursitis and plantar plate tears.

## CONDITIONS TREATED
### Overview of Conditions
Podiatry can assist with many conditions that affect not only the feet, but other lower limb conditions. Foot conditions include bunions, metatarsal stress fractures, flat foot, fungal nails, ingrown toenails, toe deformity, Morton's neuroma and plantar fasciitis. Ankle conditions include flexor hallucis longus tendonitis, posterior ankle impingement, Severs disease, shin splints and achilles tendonitis. Sports injuries and conditions include ankle sprains and knee pain. Arthritic and related conditions affecting the lower limb include osteoarthritis, diabetes and gout.
### Foot Conditions
#### Bunions
Bunion (Hallux abducto-valgus) is a large bony bump at the base of the big toe due to deviation of the big toe joint. Bunions, especially severe deformities, can often be painful to walk on. They can also cause skin irritation like callous, corns, and blisters. Larger bunions can also make finding suitable footwear challenging. Sometimes, bunion formation can lead to osteoarthritis in the big toe, which can restrict the movement at the joint, causing pain.
Treatment options include appropriate footwear with enough width and depth to accommodate the bunion; orthotic therapy, where custom or off-the-shelf foot orthosis can help improve the foot alignment and redistribute pressure under the feet and can provide a long term solution to managing painful symptoms; strapping, which provides immediate support and pain relief to the big toe joint; low level laser therapy, which helps to reduce localised joint inflammation and pain; and myofascial release therapy, which helps by reducing tightness in the muscles of the foot which support the big toe joint. Depending on the severity of the bunion, surgery may be recommended.
#### Flat Feet
Flat feet in children: it is common for young infants and toddlers to have a flat foot appearance because the arch is not fully developed. During the ages of 4 and 6 the arch will form its shape. If your child is having difficulty walking and experiencing pain, it is important to get a podiatrist to assess them. For example, they may be walking oddly or limping during walking, may get tired and want to be picked up, or may be rubbing their knees, legs, ankles or feet in a sign of pain or discomfort.
Flat feet in adults: flat feet can be acquired as an adult, generally due to injury, illness and increased stresses to the foot or from poor biomechanics. There are two types of flat feet, rigid and flexible. Other risk factors are pregnancy, obesity, hypertension, diabetes and rheumatoid arthritis. Symptoms of painful flat feet are tired feet, aching, muscle cramps and aches, knee, hip and back pain.
If you are not experiencing any pain or discomfort, then treatment may not be necessary. Usually treatment will be tailored to manage painful symptoms through physical therapies such as low level laser therapy and joint mobilisation. Orthotic devices are effective at improving the foot biomechanics. They help to lift the arch which reduces the strain on the supporting muscles and ligaments. Exercise prescription is often required to strengthen the intrinsic and extrinsic muscles of the foot. A rigid flat foot may require surgery. This is very rare, however your podiatrist will be able to assist in diagnosing this.
#### Morton's Neuroma
Morton's Neuroma is a common cause of forefoot pain. It often feels like marble is stuck in the ball of the foot. You may get a burning sensation, tingling and pins and needles in the toes. A Morton's Neuroma is an irritation of the nerve between two digits, typically between the 3rd and 4th digits. The nerve may be swollen or increased in size. It is more common in females over 35 years old.
Initial treatment options include offloading the area with orthotics, and reducing local inflammation through physical therapies such as dry needling and low level laser therapy. The podiatrist will recommend appropriate footwear options. If there is any biomechanical problem, the podiatrist will address this with their orthotic prescription or exercise prescription. Usually these options can provide significant relief. Otherwise a cortisone injection may provide temporary pain relief, although this is not a cure. Long-term management for the non-resolving Morton's Neuroma is ethanol ablation, radiofrequency ablation and neurectomy.
#### Fungal Toenails
Fungal nail infection (onychomycosis) occurs when a fungus spreads under a nail and into the nail bed causing brittle, crumbly and discoloured nails. It can spread to the nail from a nearby skin infection and affect several nails. Fungi generally thrive in hot and moist environments, which is often why it is more common in toenails rather than fingernails. Often, the main problem is cosmetic in nature due to the change in appearance of the nail. This condition rarely causes pain and odour. Fungal nail infection is usually diagnosed clinically due to its fairly distinctive appearance on the nail. Sometimes other conditions such as psoriasis can mimic fungal nail infection. If there is any doubt, you may be referred to your GP for testing of nail samples to identify the underlying cause.
A fungal infection is often stubborn to treat and recurrence is common. It can take a year before the nail starts to appear normal, but this depends on the rate of nail growth and how consistent you are with treatment. Topical antifungals are recommended if the infection is superficial or affects the end of the nail. These treatments do not always penetrate the nail plate, and as result, may not work in all cases. Oral antifungals may be recommended for infection which covers the entire nail, affects the base of the nail or is deep. These are prescribed by your GP after carefully evaluating your health status as these medications can have significant side effects.
Our clinic also offers PACT Fungal Nail Laser Therapy, an innovative and non-invasive treatment which uses photodynamic therapy to eliminate fungi in the nail. Your podiatrist can recommend a course of treatment as well as ways to prevent recurrence. Podiatrists also have special tools to reduce and manage the thickness of fungal nails. This makes it easier for the nail to absorb topical medications, improves the appearance and provides more comfort to the patient. Pactmed fungal laser therapy can improve the appearance of fungal nails in 80% of cases.
#### Plantar Fasciitis
Plantar fasciitis is inflammation of the plantar fascia and is one of the most common causes of heel pain. The plantar fascia is a thick and fibrous band of tissue on the sole of the foot connecting the heel to the toes. It provides shock absorption and stability to the foot to maintain normal motion when walking. Typically, plantar fasciitis causes a stabbing or aching pain in the inner heel or sole of the foot and is worse when taking your first steps in the morning. It can ease with walking, but a lot of people experience worsening pain after a long day standing or walking, or when standing after sitting for a long period of time.
A full biomechanical assessment should be performed to identify any risk factors. Plantar fasciitis is best managed with a combination of soft tissue therapy such as low-level laser therapy, shockwave therapy, myofascial release or dry needling, as well as exercise therapy, strapping and activity modification. Footwear assessment and modification as well as orthotics may be needed to correct contributing biomechanical issues. Your podiatrist can put together a comprehensive treatment plan suited to your needs.
#### Ingrown Toenails
Ingrown nails occur when the edges of the nail grow into the skin surrounding the nails. It is one of the most commonly seen nail conditions in a podiatry clinic. Patients often present with painful toe(s) with swelling around the affected nails, redness, pus or even an abscess.
Treatment options include removing the offending nail spicule (a procedure carried out by a podiatrist with sterile instruments); footwear changes, where good fitting shoes with a wide and deep toe box are recommended to prevent excessive pressure on the toes; and nail surgery, which permanently removes the offending portion of the nail with an in-office, minimally invasive surgical procedure and is recommended for recurrent cases.
### Ankle Conditions
#### Achilles Tendonitis
Achilles tendinopathy or tendinitis is one of the most common overuse injuries that causes pain, swelling, stiffness and weakness of the Achilles tendon. The Achilles tendon is the largest tendon in the body which connects the calf muscle at the back of the lower leg to the heel bone. It is commonly seen in runners, ballet dancers or athletes whose sports include repetitive jumping movements. People suffering with achilles tendinopathy usually experience pain along the mid portion and or insertional area of the tendon at the back of the heel bone.
The conservative treatment options depend on the severity of the injury. Options include rest and activity modification to allow injured tissue sufficient recovery time; addressing contributing biomechanical factors via orthotics and footwear modifications; physical therapy with a home stretch and strengthening program to improve the tendon's tolerance to load; manual therapy including myofascial release, joint mobilisation or taping to improve the range and quality of movement; and adjunct therapy such as low level laser therapy and shockwave therapy. Conservative therapy should be instigated for 3-6 months before considering other therapy such as injection therapy or surgery.
#### Severs Disease
Sever's disease is a condition which can result in debilitating heel pain. It affects kids and adolescents typically between the ages of 8 and 14. The condition is also known as calcaneal apophysitis, as there is inflammation and irritation of the growth plate (apophysis) in the heel bone (calcaneus). This affliction is more common in kids who are highly active and participate in a lot of high-impact physical activity which involve running and jumping such as Aussie Rules Football or basketball. Kids may complain of pain during or after sport in the heel, instep and calf. They may also limp or toe walk to try to alleviate their symptoms.
What our podiatrists will do when they suspect Sever's is to perform a detailed biomechanical assessment in order to formulate a clinical diagnosis, as medical imaging is rarely indicated. From there, our discussions will provide a bespoke treatment plan based on the child's activity and presentation. This includes discussing load management on the heels, a return to activity plan, strapping to offload, exercise rehabilitation, heel lifts, appropriate footwear recommendations and modifications, dry needling and massage, over-the-counter or custom foot orthoses if needed, and low level laser therapy.
### Sports Injuries and Conditions
#### Ankle Sprains
There are many grades of ankle sprain, however if you cannot bear weight after a sprain, it is time to go straight to the emergency department. After an ankle sprain, you may feel pain on the outside of the ankle and notice swelling and bruising. You may also find walking is difficult. Seeing a podiatrist early after an ankle sprain can help manage painful symptoms and promote faster healing. This is done by having a thorough assessment to determine the degree of injury and treatment which may include low level laser which stimulates tissue repair and reduces swelling. Appropriate rehabilitation of the ankle is crucial in preventing recurrent ankle instability; this can include graded exercises for proprioception, strength and flexibility.
#### Knee Pain
Knee pain can be caused by any of the bony structures comprising the knee joint (femur, tibia, fibula), the kneecap (patella), ligaments, tendons, and cartilage (menisci) of the knee. The three main risk factors of knee pain are overuse, biomechanics, and excessive stress. Knee pain can be reduced by the alteration of biomechanics for a more even distribution of pressure throughout the joint. If the knee pain has been present for more than a week, it is time to book an appointment to get assessed.
Treatment includes physical therapy, where stretching and strengthening the muscles around the knees helps increase joint mobility and stabilising the knees; orthotic therapy, which when knee pain is associated with abnormal biomechanics or conditions such as osteoarthritis can help by correcting the alignment of the foot and lower limb and shifting pressure away from the sides of the knees; and low level laser therapy, which helps by reducing localised inflammation and swelling and provides an analgesic effect.
### Arthritis and Diabetes
#### Osteoarthritis
Osteoarthritis (OA) is the most common form of arthritis. It is understood to be a form of degeneration within a joint which is caused by mechanical stress. Symptoms include pain, tenderness, stiffness and mild swelling. The pain is often described as a sharp ache or constant low grade pain. Osteoarthritis can affect all areas of the body including the feet, hands, knees, hips and spine. In the feet, it contributes to the formation of bunions. Diagnosis is based on history and clinical examination. An x-ray can assist in the diagnosis.
Tailored exercise programs can improve the function and pain for OA in the hip and knee. The use of physical therapies such as dry needling and low level laser therapy can reduce localised pain and swelling. Orthotic therapy can be useful to provide cushioning and offloading of painful joints in the feet. Different orthotic wedging can also affect knee and hip osteoarthritis. Your podiatrist will recommend footwear to assist with foot deformity caused by osteoarthritis. Other treatment options include steroid injections, pain medication and surgery, which can be discussed with your podiatrist. There is substantial evidence to support the use of low level laser therapy in treating OA along with exercise. We routinely use low level laser therapy as a primary treatment option to manage painful arthritic presentations.
#### Diabetes and Foot Health
Diabetes is a serious chronic health condition which occurs when there is too much glucose in the blood. Diabetes can cause significant complications for the feet and legs. Feet are at risk because diabetes can cause damage to the nerves and the blood supply. Everyone with diabetes should arrange to have regular foot examinations by a podiatrist. If you have diabetes, it is recommended you have a foot check or assessment at least once a year. If you have any complications, your feet should be checked every 3-6 months. A visit to a podiatrist will also show you how to check and care for your feet. Our podiatrists will assess your footwear, functional range of motion, muscle strength and your skin and nails.
People with diabetes are more at risk of developing blockages in their arteries or hardening of the arteries. Symptoms include sharp leg cramps in the calves after walking short distances or uphill, cold feet, pale skin colour, no hair on the feet, shiny and taut skin, and cuts or injuries which are slow to heal. If you have any of these signs or symptoms, we recommend you see a podiatrist or health care practitioner for further assessment.
Poor blood glucose control can lead to nerve damage. The nerves affected are generally your sensory nerves (the ability to feel), those which supply your muscles and those which supply the sweat glands. The symptoms are pins and needles or tingling in the feet and or legs, numbness in the feet, burning pain in the legs and feet often felt at night, gradually worsening hammer toes and loss of muscle tone in the forefoot, and drier skin on the feet. Often the nerve damage goes unnoticed and you may not realise you have nerve damage until you go for a foot check. Nerve damage increases the risk of accidental injury to the feet, which can develop into an ulcer or sore on the foot.
