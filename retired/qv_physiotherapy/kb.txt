## YOUR FIRST PHYSIOTHERAPY VISIT

### Referral

You do not need a GP referral to see a physio. If your doctor has referred you, the clinic keeps them updated on your diagnosis and progress.

### Session Length

The initial consultation is a full 45 minutes. Follow-up appointments are 30 minutes, longer for complex issues or concurrent problems.

### What Happens

Your physio takes your history, assesses how you move and explains what is driving your pain before treatment starts. You are treated in the same session with hands-on manual therapy, dry needling and guided exercise matched to your injury. You leave with exercises to do and an idea of how many sessions you will need. The physio advises how many sessions after the initial consultation, depending on your condition and how you respond to treatment. Your program is accessible from your phone.

### What to Wear

Come in your regular clothes. You can bring a singlet or shorts to change into, and the clinic has shorts, shirts and singlets on site if needed.

## PHYSIOTHERAPY

### Approach

All sessions are 30 to 45 minutes and focus on active therapy: hands-on treatment, dry needling, guided exercises, stretching and rehabilitation.

### Dry Needling

Dry needling uses a sterile, single-use filament needle or acupuncture needle to relieve muscle tension, reduce pain and stimulate circulation with minimal pain. The physiotherapist explains any risks before treatment.

### Manual Therapies

Manual therapy includes mobilisation, manipulation, massage, trigger point therapy, stretching, myofascial release and muscle re-education.

### Rehabilitation Therapies

Rehabilitation includes muscle strengthening, conditioning programs, stretching programs, core training and Pilates, balance retraining, plyometrics, fitball work and post-surgical strengthening.

## REMEDIAL MASSAGE

### About

Remedial massage is an assessment-led treatment using targeted manual techniques for muscle pain, tension and restricted movement. The therapist assesses posture, movement, painful areas and injury history, then builds a treatment plan that may include manual pressure, trigger point therapy and other techniques. It can be combined with physiotherapy when pain needs exercise prescription or further assessment. Massage is often used for muscle tightness and pain, arthritis, frozen shoulder, tennis elbow, whiplash, neck and back pain, scoliosis, headaches and sports injuries.

### Types of Massage

#### Remedial Massage
A form of deep tissue massage for knotted, tense or damaged muscles, tendons and joints. It includes clinical assessment and targeted treatment, unlike general relaxation massage.
#### Deep Tissue Massage
Firm, slow strokes and sustained pressure reach deeper muscle and connective tissue, for persistent tension and recurring knots.
#### Pregnancy Massage
Uses modified positions and techniques for muscle pain and tension as the body changes. The clinic offers pregnancy massage up to 29 weeks gestation and asks clients to get approval from their GP beforehand. Beyond that, a women's health physio may be more suitable.
#### Relaxation Massage
A non-clinical option using flowing techniques and lighter pressure.
#### Lymphatic Drainage
Available as a 60 minute session.

### Massage Prices

### First Massage Appointment

The therapist asks about symptoms, lifestyle, health history and goals and may do a physical assessment. Wear comfortable clothing, as you only uncover the area being treated and appropriate draping is used. The therapist may suggest stretches or corrective exercises afterwards.

### Massage FAQ

#### What is the difference between a massage and a remedial massage?
General massage often focuses on relaxation. Remedial massage includes an assessment and targeted treatment for pain, tension, injury or restricted movement.
#### How often should I have a remedial massage?
It depends on your condition, and the therapists can advise.
#### Can I have massage after injury or surgery?
Once treatment has been approved by your doctor or physiotherapist, remedial massage may help manage scar tissue, muscle guarding and stiffness after injury or surgery. If you have chronic pain, weakness, recurring injury or pain radiating into the leg, the therapist may recommend physiotherapy alongside massage.

## WOMEN'S HEALTH AND PELVIC FLOOR PHYSIOTHERAPY

### About

Women's health physiotherapists support women through pregnancy, birth, recovery and later stages of life. Lauren Kendall treats pregnancy, postnatal, pelvic floor and continence conditions. All appointments take place in a private treatment room, and treatment is confidential and sensitive.

### Conditions Treated

Urinary incontinence or sudden bladder urgency, overactive bladder, faecal incontinence, prolapse and pelvic heaviness, pelvic pain, sexual pain and painful vaginal symptoms, endometriosis-related pain, pain with intimacy, bowel issues and constipation, perineal tears following delivery, sexual dysfunction, core weakness after pregnancy or surgery, and pelvic floor recovery after pregnancy and birth. For women in perimenopause and menopause, the physiotherapist can address pelvic health, continence, muscle strength and painful symptoms.

### Treatments

Pelvic floor retraining using Kegel exercises, real-time ultrasound and muscle stimulation, manual therapy for tight or painful pelvic floor muscles, bladder training for overactive bladder, and safe return to exercise after gynaecology surgery. Bladder issues are typically treated with 3 months of supervised pelvic floor strengthening. Chronic pelvic pain involves pain science, healthy lifestyle habits and down-training pelvic floor sensitivity.

### Pregnancy and Postnatal Physiotherapy

Pregnancy physio treats pelvic girdle pain and other pregnancy-related pain through exercise, joint mobilisation, dry needling or pregnancy massage, and can cover breathing, relaxation, labour positions and TENS education. Pelvic floor training during pregnancy can reduce postnatal incontinence. Postnatal treatment may include pelvic floor re-education, ultrasound-guided abdominal retraining, diastasis (abdominal separation) or caesarean recovery, and advice on breastfeeding and carrying positions. Book the postnatal check about six weeks after birth, or earlier if advised by your healthcare provider. The physio builds a gradual return-to-exercise plan, and further support is available through clinical Pilates and clinical exercise for women.

### Women's Health Prices

Women's Health Initial (45 minutes): $140. Post Natal Check (1 hour): $150. Women's Health Follow Up (30 minutes): $100. Follow Up Long Session (45 minutes): $125.

### Women's Health FAQ

#### Do I need a referral?
No referral is needed to see a pelvic floor physiotherapist, though letters or reports from your treating doctor or health professional are helpful.
#### How long is an appointment?
Standard initial pelvic floor and prenatal appointments are 45 minutes. Postnatal checks are 1 hour. For a complex condition or multiple issues, ask reception for a 1 hour appointment.
#### Do I need an internal assessment?
No. An internal assessment is only recommended if relevant, and you can decline at any time. Your physiotherapist should always tell you the alternatives, and your participation in any part of the assessment is your choice. If you are not comfortable, a real-time ultrasound is used instead, with a probe placed externally on the skin just above the pubic bone.
#### Can I have an internal assessment if I have my period?
Yes, it is your choice. The physiotherapist always wears gloves and protective equipment and it will not affect the assessment.
#### Can I use a Chronic Disease Management Care Plan?
Yes, EPC care plans are welcome for chronic conditions lasting more than 3 months, and you can claim your rebate back directly with the clinic.
#### Do you accept private health insurance?
All pelvic health physiotherapy services are rebatable with private health extras. The rebate depends on your level of cover, so check with your provider.
#### What is a pelvic floor physiotherapist?
A physiotherapist registered with AHPRA who has undertaken additional training to treat conditions of the pelvic floor.
#### What happens in the initial appointment?
The physio takes a comprehensive history covering bladder, bowel, gynae, sexual, obstetric and general health, and your goals. They assess your pelvis, abdominals, spine, hips and breathing, which may take 1 to 2 sessions depending on complexity. The pelvic floor assessment can be internal (a lubricated, gloved finger inserted into the vagina or rectum, which should be pain free) or by real-time ultrasound. The physio then explains findings and creates a management plan with you.

## CLINICAL PILATES (CLINICAL REHAB)

### About

Clinical Pilates is physiotherapist-led exercise tailored to your injury, condition and movement needs, developed and supervised by physiotherapists who understand your injury history and goals. It retrains stabilising muscles that support the spine, hips and joints, and improves core strength, posture, flexibility and coordination. Equipment may include a Pilates reformer, trapeze table, Wunda chair, mat or small apparatus.

### Who It Is For

Rehabilitation after injury or surgery, ongoing lower back and neck pain, chronic pain management, injury prevention, general conditioning, and pre and postnatal clients.

### Group Classes

Group classes run for 45 minutes with a maximum of four clients. Each person follows an individual program under supervision.

### Private Reformer Sessions

A private one-to-one reformer session with your physiotherapist lasts 30 minutes. It may suit a recent or complex injury or anyone who prefers not to join a group.

### Clinical Pilates FAQ

#### Do I need a referral for clinical Pilates?
No referral is needed, but you may need an initial assessment before joining classes so the physiotherapist can create a safe program.
#### What is the difference between Pilates and clinical Pilates?
General Pilates classes usually follow a shared routine led by an instructor. Clinical Pilates is supervised by a physiotherapist and uses tailored exercise programs for the individual.
#### How is my program built?
An initial assessment covers symptoms, injury history, diagnosis, fitness and goals plus posture, strength, mobility and movement. The physiotherapist creates an individual program, checks your technique each session, and reassesses as your strength improves.

## GLA:D PROGRAM (GOOD LIFE WITH ARTHRITIS: DENMARK)

GLA:D is an education and exercise program developed by researchers in Denmark for people with hip or knee osteoarthritis symptoms. GLA:D Australia is for anyone with hip and/or knee osteoarthritis symptoms regardless of severity, if the hip or knee problem has led them to visit a health care provider.

### Who May Not Be Able to Participate

People whose hip or knee pain has other causes such as tumour, inflammatory joint disease, result of hip fracture, or soft or connective tissue problems, people whose other symptoms are more pronounced than the osteoarthritis (for example chronic generalised pain or fibromyalgia), and people who cannot understand and communicate in English.
