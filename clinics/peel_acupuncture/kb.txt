# Peel Acupuncture & Traditional Chinese Medicine

## About
### Practitioner
My name is Simon Wright and I am a Chinese Medicine Board of Australia (CMBA) registered acupuncturist with 20+ years of experience working in the field of Traditional Chinese Medicine (TCM).

My professional interests are in treating infertility and pregnancy issues, stress and anxiety and all types of pain, whether it be acute, subacute or chronic pain.

## Services
### Treatment Approach
Peel Acupuncture predominantly uses traditional Chinese acupuncture in treatments, but depending on the presenting condition, I will often incorporate western style techniques like dry needling to release active trigger points and motor points.

#### Cupping
Cupping or functional release cupping is often used alongside acupuncture to improve treatment outcomes. Dysfunction within the fascial system can produce significant mobility issues and pain, and cupping is able to release problematic superficial fascia.

#### Gua Sha
Gua Sha is a technique of applying a scrubbing type of massage with a smooth blunt edged tool.

I will often use Gua Sha prior to an acupuncture or cupping treatment if there is some form of fascial dysfunction.

#### Moxibustion And Infrared Therapy
Heat treatments using Moxibustion or an Infrared heat lamp are often combined with other TCM techniques to aid healing. Moxibustion is also traditionally used to assist in the case of a breech presentation in the third trimester of pregnancy.

### Preparing For Your Treatment
If you have relevant reports from X-rays, ultrasounds, CT or MRI scans, please bring them along with you as they will give me a better understanding of your current problems.

Do make sure you have eaten a light meal within 2 to 3 hours of your appointment and also make sure you are well hydrated.

Usually loose clothing allowing easy access to your limbs and trunk is sufficient, but if other areas need to be exposed, towels will be provided to protect your modesty.

## Infertility
### Acupuncture And Infertility
Acupuncture and TCM can be useful in reducing stress levels, helping to regulate the menstrual cycle and in dealing with any premenstrual symptoms or period pain.

Acupuncture can also be used in conjunction with assisted reproductive technologies (A.R.T.) from western fertility clinics (e.g. IVF treatments).

## FAQ
#### Does Acupuncture Hurt?
Extremely sharp fine needles are inserted into the skin using a guide tube ensuring that for most people acupuncture is a relatively painless procedure.

#### Are The Benefits Of Acupuncture Immediate?
For the average patient, it may take 48 hours to notice the benefits of acupuncture. It is very important for you to set realistic goals depending on whether you have an acute, subacute or chronic condition. Usually patients with acute or subacute conditions will achieve faster results than those with chronic conditions.

#### How Long Are The Needles Retained For?
The needles are usually retained for about 20 minutes but in some circumstances, it may only be a few minutes and in other cases up to 30 minutes.

#### Are There Side Effects?
The main side effect of acupuncture is a sense of relaxation or tiredness which may last for a few minutes or even a few hours after treatment. Sometimes a patient may feel slightly dizzy when getting up after acupuncture but that normally passes within a few minutes.

#### Do You Need A Referral To See An Acupuncturist?
No referral is needed to consult with an acupuncturist.

#### Can I Choose What Technique I Want Done?
The practitioner will determine the best course of action for your treatment but special requests regarding preferred techniques will certainly be considered.

#### Are The Needles Sterile?
Yes, single use disposable needles are used each and every time.
