# Cath Lilburn – Herbalist and Naturopath

## About Cath Lilburn

Cath Lilburn is a naturopathic herbalist based in Bulli, Illawarra, with over 25 years of clinical experience at the intersection of traditional herbal wisdom and evidence-based practice.

Her clinical foundation was shaped by the late Dorothy Hall, one of Australia's most respected traditional herbalists. She later completed a Bachelor of Medicines Management with Professional Honours in Complementary Medicine through the University of Tasmania. She continues advanced clinical education including specialist women's health mentoring in perimenopause and menopause, and advanced microbiome and gastrointestinal training with Dr Jason Hawrelak.

She has a sub-specialty supporting women with breast cancer recovery and in the therapeutic use of medicinal mushrooms including Reishi. Her published research in this area was recognised by the NHAA and published in the Journal of the Naturopaths and Herbalists Association of Australia in 2021, as a finalist for article of the year. In 2024, she became an Australian Ambassador for the Integrative and Naturopathic Medicine World Congress.

## Perimenopause and Menopause

### Overview

Perimenopause is the five to eight years leading up to menopause, defined as 12 months after the last menstrual period. It is a time of hormonal transition that can greatly affect mood, sleep, the nervous system, and overall energy.

### Symptoms

Common symptoms include mood changes, menstrual changes and irregularities, sleep disruption, headaches and migraines, energy shifts, and weight gain.

### How Cath Can Help

Natural therapies including herbal tonics, targeted nutrients, and practical lifestyle adjustments can support this transition while addressing long-term wellbeing.

Specific areas of support include sleep and nervous system changes, mood and anxiety and emotional resilience, changing cycles and premenstrual symptoms, fatigue and weight and metabolic changes, digestive and microbiome health, and bone, cardiovascular and healthy ageing foundations. Cath also works collaboratively with a GP when Menopausal Hormone Therapy or further medical investigation may be appropriate.

## Breast Cancer Support

### Overview

Cath has particular depth of experience supporting women through breast cancer from diagnosis through treatment and into recovery. Support includes nutrition and resilience during treatment with all recommendations considered alongside oncology care, managing treatment side effects naturopathically and safely alongside oncology care, rebuilding strength and energy and resilience once active treatment ends, and longer-term support for recovery and overall health and vitality.

Physical symptoms such as post-cancer fatigue are very common following treatment. Individualised herbal tonics combined with lifestyle adjustments and focused nutritional support are helpful in this space.

### Clinical Background

Cath first studied cancer support strategies in 2000 with Herbalist Nancy Evelyn in Newcastle. In 2018, she completed integrative oncology study with American Naturopath Lise Alschuler. Her 2021 University of Tasmania research focused on medicinal mushrooms as a potential adjunct treatment for breast cancer patients following oncology treatments, published in the NHAA Journal and a finalist for article of the year.

### Support During Diagnosis

The period right after diagnosis involves absorbing frightening news while making significant decisions about surgery and treatment. Common experiences include difficulty thinking clearly, disrupted sleep, and overwhelming anxiety. Nervous system support at this stage helps steady the mind, improve sleep, and build resilience through appointments and decisions.

### Support During Treatment

During surgery, chemotherapy, and radiation, naturopathic support can help with preparing for surgery and supporting recovery, managing side effects such as nausea, fatigue and skin sensitivity, supporting immune resilience, and steadying anxiety and sleep. This works alongside the oncology team.

### Support After Treatment Ends

Many women find themselves without a clear map after active treatment ends. Ongoing fatigue, brain fog, disrupted sleep, and anxiety about recurrence are all common. Naturopathic support for recovery includes targeted nutritional support and individualised herbal tonics to address post-treatment fatigue, immune resilience support drawing on research into medicinal mushrooms such as Reishi, nervous system support through herbal medicine and nutrition and lifestyle changes, and a structured recovery plan to help restore a sense of agency.
