## About Osteopathy and Chiropractic

Osteopathy and Chiropractic are forms of manual medicine focusing on the relationship between structure and function of the body. Osteopathy is derived from the Greek words for bone and disease; Chiropractic from the Greek words for hand and done.

Both approaches are hands-on, non-invasive treatments that help reduce back and neck pain, improve movement and relieve muscle tension without relying on medication. No referral is needed as we are primary healthcare practitioners.

Treatment may include adjustments, myofascial release, spinal traction, dry needling acupuncture, instrument-assisted soft tissue mobilisation, fascia release, active release, trigger point therapy, and cupping therapy. We also provide tailored advice on rehabilitation exercises, posture and ergonomics.

Research supports spinal manipulation for chronic back pain, with improvements in both pain and function. Evidence also suggests osteopathic and chiropractic care, including spinal adjustments, may help reduce the frequency, intensity and duration of migraines while lowering reliance on pain medication.

## About Dry Needling and Western Acupuncture

Dry Needling and Western Acupuncture use fine needles (approximately 10 times smaller than vaccination needles) at specific point locations to facilitate blood circulation and harmonise the nervous system. The treatment should be a painless experience.

We often use dry needling in conjunction with osteopathic and chiropractic techniques, finding it effective for acute and chronic musculoskeletal conditions. It can also assist with systemic conditions such as depression and insomnia.

Research suggests dry needling is more effective when combined with chiropractic care, helping improve outcomes for patients with low back pain. It can also be used as a standalone treatment to reduce muscle tension, improve movement and support recovery.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### What is osteopathic and chiropractic care?

Osteopathic and chiropractic care are hands-on healthcare approaches that focus on root cause diagnosis and treating musculoskeletal conditions affecting the spine, joints, muscles, and nervous system. No referral is needed as we are primary healthcare practitioners.

#### What happens during the initial consultation?

The first appointment typically includes a detailed medical history review, discussion of symptoms and goals, postural and movement assessment, physical examination, and possibly imaging, followed by a diagnosis and treatment plan. Treatment may begin during the first visit and may include adjustments, dry needle acupuncture, fascia release, active release and rehabilitation advice.

#### Are osteopathic and chiropractic adjustments safe?

Yes. Osteopathic and Chiropractic treatments are considered safe and effective when performed by qualified practitioners. Practitioners hold a Bachelor's Degree and Masters, with training grounded in evidence-based research. Techniques are always adapted to suit your age, medical history, comfort level and treatment goals. We also offer alternative treatments for those who do not choose to have adjustments.

#### Do adjustments hurt?

Most patients find adjustments comfortable and relieving rather than painful. You may feel gentle pressure or movement during an adjustment. After treatment you may feel mild soreness similar to post-exercise muscle soreness, which usually settles within 24 to 48 hours.

#### Will adjustments make my bones crack?

The sound heard during an adjustment is caused by small gas bubbles releasing within the joint fluid as pressure changes, similar to clicking your knuckles. This is completely normal and painless. Not all treatments involve this and we can employ many other techniques that are more comfortable if preferred.

#### How many treatments will I need?

The number of treatments varies depending on your condition, how long the problem has been present, your activity levels and lifestyle, and your treatment goals. Most patients experience noticeable relief in the first visit, while others benefit from a course of care to fully resolve the issue and prevent recurrence. In general, the sooner you come in, the faster you will heal.

#### Is osteopathic and chiropractic care only for back and neck pain?

No. While chiropractors commonly treat back and neck pain, we also help with headaches, migraines and TMJ pain, postural problems, shoulder and hip pain, elbow and knee pain, sports injuries, and joint stiffness and mobility issues.

#### Can osteopathic and chiropractic care improve posture?

Yes. Treatment improves spinal mobility, reduces muscle tension, corrects movement patterns, and we provide posture and ergonomic advice along with strengthening exercises.

#### Do you prescribe exercises or rehabilitation?

Yes. Exercise and rehabilitation are an integral part of treatment. We aim to find the root cause and provide the relevant strengthening steps to reduce the risk of re-injury and promote lasting results. Exercises are tailored to your individual condition and goals.

#### Can children or pregnant women receive osteopathic and chiropractic care?

Yes. Care can be adapted for all ages, including children and pregnant women. Practitioners use gentle, modified techniques. Many people seek care during pregnancy for back pain, pelvic discomfort, and posture changes.

#### Is treatment covered by private health insurance and do I need a referral?

In most cases, private health insurance with extras cover includes osteopathic and chiropractic treatment. Check your level of cover with your health fund. No referral is needed to make an appointment, though a GP referral is required for Medicare rebates under the Chronic Condition Management Plan.

## Back and Neck Pain for Parents

Parenting places constant physical demands on the body. Lifting children and carrying prams, working at a desk, and dealing with interrupted sleep commonly cause back and neck pain to build over time.

Common conditions we treat for parents include lower back pain from lifting children, neck pain and stiffness from feeding or carrying, shoulder and upper back tension, postural headaches, and hip pain from uneven weight distribution.

Contributing factors include lifting and carrying children multiple times per day, holding babies in one position for extended periods, feeding positions that strain the neck and upper back, pushing prams and managing car seats, poor sleep, and long hours at a desk.

Treatment for parents focuses on reducing strain, improving movement, and helping the body cope with physical demands. Care may include joint adjustments and mobilisation, muscle and fascial release, dry needling acupuncture, postural and ergonomic advice, and simple exercises that fit into a busy routine.

## Desk Worker Back and Neck Pain

Long periods of sitting or standing can lead to reduced spinal mobility, muscle imbalance, increased joint stiffness, postural strain from screens and laptops, recurring headaches and neck tension, and lower back and hip tightness.

Common problems we treat for desk workers include neck pain from computer work, persistent lower back pain, shoulder and upper back tension including rounded shoulders, sciatica and hip stiffness, wrist and forearm discomfort from typing, and stress-related muscle tension.

Treatment for desk professionals focuses on restoring movement and reducing strain. This includes gentle adjustments and joint mobilisation, muscle and fascia release, postural assessment and ergonomic advice, and simple mobility exercises designed for office routines.

#### What is the best sitting posture?

Sitting with the chest upright is best. Some people do this better using the chair's back rest. This position activates the hips, glutes and mid-back, reducing overuse of the lower back and upper trapezius muscles.

#### How often should I get up from sitting?

It is best to get up every 30 to 60 minutes. A five-minute walk break between tasks helps restore circulation.

#### What exercises can I do to reduce desk-related pain?

Air squats help get blood pumping back into the hips and glutes and away from the lower back. Shoulder blade squeeze holds help restore circulation to the mid-back.

#### What is the difference between chiropractic and osteopathy?

Both focus on improving movement and reducing pain through hands-on care. Traditional chiropractic often emphasises spinal adjustments, while osteopathy may include broader soft tissue work such as muscle release, fascia release and movement-based approaches. The most important factor is choosing a practitioner who tailors treatment to your individual needs.
