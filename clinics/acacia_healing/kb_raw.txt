Manual lymphatic drainage (MLD) is a gentle, rhythmic massage technique that supports the natural movement of lymph fluid through the body. It encourages lymph to flow toward lymph nodes, where waste and toxins can be filtered and cleared. This process helps reduce swelling, supports detoxification, enhances immune function, and promotes deep relaxation.

This treatment may be beneficial for:

Post-surgical recovery

Fluid retention

Reducing inflammation

Supporting immune health

General wellbeing and relaxation

MLD is intended as a gentle “refresh” for an otherwise healthy lymphatic system.

Important information – Please read before booking

A current medical clearance from your doctor is required before receiving this treatment.

Even if you’ve received clearance in the past, an updated clearance is essential to ensure that lymphatic drainage is safe and appropriate for your needs.

Lymphatic drainage is not suitable for individuals with the following conditions:

Kidney disease or renal failure

Congestive heart conditions

Diabetes

Malignant cancers

Skin infections (e.g. cellulitis, ulcers)

Thrombosis or blood clots

Lymphoedema (or suspected lymphoedema)

If you experience symptoms such as persistent swelling, warmth at the site, skin discolouration, thickening, or pitting, please speak to your doctor before booking. These may be signs of lymphoedema, which requires a separate medical assessment and clearance.

FAQs

Do I need a referral or medical clearance to book?

Yes. A current medical clearance from your doctor is required before receiving any lymphatic drainage massage. This helps ensure the treatment is safe for your specific health needs, particularly if you have underlying conditions or recent medical history.

Why is medical clearance required?

While manual lymphatic drainage is gentle, it can interfere with some health conditions. Issues such as kidney disease, heart problems, diabetes, certain cancers, infections, and blood clots can make MLD unsafe without medical supervision. If you experience symptoms of lymphoedema (like persistent swelling or skin changes), you must be assessed by your doctor before booking.

What happens during the Initial Consultation?

The 75-minute initial session includes a health history review, discussion of your goals, and a tailored treatment plan. Manon will ensure that MLD is appropriate for your needs and begin treatment if clearance has been provided and no contraindications are present.

Is lymphatic drainage painful?

Not at all. MLD is a very light, flowing treatment. The pressure is soft and gentle – this is intentional and appropriate for the lymphatic system, which sits close to the surface of the skin.

How many sessions will I need?

This depends on your individual needs. Some clients find a single session helpful for general wellness, while others may benefit from a series of treatments post-surgery or to manage fluid retention. Manon can guide you during your initial appointment.

Can I book a session if I haven’t had surgery or a medical condition?

Yes, absolutely. Lymphatic drainage can support general wellness, immune health, and relaxation. It’s also popular during seasonal changes or times of physical or emotional stress.

What is Clinical Nutrition?

Clinical Nutrition is the art and science of using food as medicine to restore balance, prevent disease, and support the body’s natural ability to heal. From a functional medicine perspective, Clinical Nutritionists look beyond symptoms to uncover the root causes of imbalance, recognising that every body is unique.

We use tools such as functional pathology and nutrigenomics when appropriate, and we prioritise multi-modality, integrative care for every client. By combining evidence-based nutrition with lifestyle, environmental, and individual factors, this approach represents the future of healthcare. Care that is personalised, preventative, and powerful.

Although the term “Nutritionist” isn’t protected by registration in Australia, Bachelor-qualified Clinical Nutritionists are clinically trained to practice nutritional medicine and to treat disease across all life stages. We are qualified to use food as medicine, along with targeted nutritional therapeutics and lifestyle interventions, to support disease prevention and treatment, and overall health optimisation.

The human body has an incredible capacity to heal itself. When the foundations are in place, it naturally moves toward balance. Sometimes, targeted support is needed to restore that process. From conception planning through to adulthood, nourishment is vital for growth, repair, and lifelong vitality.

Nutrition might just be the medicine you didn’t know you needed.

What It’s Not

Clinical Nutrition is not about calorie counting or fad diets. It doesn’t involve restriction, guilt, or one-size-fits-all generic meal plans. Instead, it’s a science-based, individualised approach that seeks to understand the
why
behind your symptoms. This empowers you to create lasting health through evidence, education, and genuine support.

What is a sliding scale?

A sliding scale is a self-selected scale and way to charge for something that honours the fact that folks have different access to resources- financial and otherwise. It is a way that we attempt to create equity in folks being able to access services and/or goods. The basic idea is that each person self-selects for themselves what they will pay from the tiered options given. Folks that are able to pay on the higher end support folks with less resources to pay at the lower end, without the clinician having to compromise making a livelihood. Paying on the higher end of the scale is an act of solidarity, where you can directly enable access to care for folks less privileged than you. Paying on the lower end of the scale honours a recognition that you experience less privilege or are currently under resourced in your life. Where you select on the scale isn’t necessarily permanent and may change at different times or under different life circumstances – it is fluid. i.e. you’re unemployed in the lead up to the first appointment, but by the 3rd appointment, you are earning an income. We encourage you to be honest with yourself about what you can afford, keeping in mind that a sliding scale only works when folks self-select across the whole scale. This isn’t about what you ‘want’ to pay for this service, but is more about what you can reasonably afford without impacting your ability to pay rent, bills, groceries etc. It can be confronting looking at your privilege and deciding where to pay on the scale, if you need help feel free to contact Monica at

Now that you are beginning a series of Structural Integration sessions, it can help to approach the process as a project rather than a one-off treatment.

This work is not simply about relieving symptoms. It is a structured process aimed at reorganising how your body is supported in gravity, improving breathing, restoring movement efficiency, and reducing unnecessary strain.

Below are suggestions to help you get the most from your sessions. Not all of them will apply to you. Take what feels useful and leave the rest.

Getting the Most From Your Structural Integration Sessions

1. Consider Keeping a Journal

Structural work often creates subtle and cumulative changes.

You may notice shifts in:

Posture

Movement

Energy levels

Mood or emotional tone

Sleep patterns

Sensations in areas that previously felt “neutral”

Before your first session, stand in front of a mirror and simply observe yourself. Write down what you see and feel. Where do you feel balanced? Where do you feel tension? What aches are present? What do you like about your body? What feels unfamiliar?

Make brief notes before and after sessions. Many clients are surprised by how much has changed when they look back.

2. Allow Time to Feel the Changes

If possible, leave a few minutes before and after your session to walk.

Walking helps you:

Notice the “before” and “after”

Integrate changes in a functional way

Allow your nervous system to settle

Clear your head before returning to driving or daily demands

You might choose to park a short distance away and walk to the clinic.

3. Stay Present During the Work

Structural Integration is interactive. You will often be asked to move body parts while they are being worked on.

The most productive sessions happen when you:

Stay aware of the area being worked

Breathe normally

Communicate clearly

It is very common to tighten or pull away when sensation increases. If pressure feels too strong, too fast, or too much, say so. The goal is effective change, not endurance.

Some areas may feel intense, particularly where there has been past injury or long-held restriction. Intensity can be productive, but dissociation is not. If you feel yourself mentally “leaving,” the pressure is likely too much.

This is your session. The best results come from collaboration.

4. Listen to Your Body Between Sessions

Between appointments, your body is adapting.

You may feel:

Restless and wanting to move more

Tired and needing extra rest

Mild unfamiliar strains as forces redistribute

All of this can be normal.

If you feel the urge to move, move. Walk, stretch, dance, breathe deeply.

If you feel the need to rest, rest.

If anything feels sharp, severe, or persists beyond a few days, let Chris know.

5. Break Up Long Periods of Sitting

Long-held sitting patterns are one of the most powerful drivers of structural compensation.

Between sessions:

Stand and move every hour if possible

Adjust your workstation

Change how you stand at the sink

Vary how you sit and drive

Small, frequent changes are more effective than rigid corrections.

6. Avoid Forcing “Good Posture”

Do not try to hold yourself in what you think is correct posture.

Pinning your shoulders back.

Tucking your pelvis under.

Holding your chest up artificially.

These efforts often create new strain patterns.

The series is designed to help your body find a more efficient balance without force. Allow your posture to reorganise rather than imposing an ideal onto it.

Your body has its own blueprint.

7. Notice Old Patterns Returning

Old holding patterns often reappear.

Your role is not to fight them.

It is simply to notice them.

When you catch yourself gripping, bracing, collapsing, or clenching, gently let it go. No drama. No correction. Just release.

Repeated noticing gradually weakens the old pattern until the new organisation feels natural.

8. Warm Baths & Recovery

A warm bath in the evening after a session can feel restorative.

Epsom salts may reduce residual soreness.

Hydration is also essential. Fascia responds best when adequately hydrated.

9. Emotional Shifts

Occasionally, people notice emotional shifts during a series.

This is not unusual. The body and nervous system are closely linked.

If emotions arise, observe them without over-analysing.

Anything persistent or concerning should be discussed.

10. Trust the Process

The changes you feel immediately after a session may seem less dramatic as the week progresses. This does not mean they are gone. It usually means they have been integrated.

The 3-Series and 12-Series follow a deliberate progression. Each session builds on the last to reduce regression and support lasting change.

Many clients continue to notice positive changes in the months following completion of a series.

Finally

Enjoy the process.

Structural Integration is an opportunity to explore your body in a different way. It is about reclaiming ease, efficiency, and a more comfortable relationship with gravity.

Chris looks forward to guiding you through the process.
