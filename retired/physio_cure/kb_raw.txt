Appointments & your first visit

Do I need a referral?

No referral is generally needed for a private
physiotherapy appointment. A referral or supporting
documents may be required for Medicare, DVA, TAC,
WorkSafe and some other funded pathways.

What happens at my first appointment?

Your physiotherapist will discuss your symptoms,
health history and goals, then assess your movement
and function. They will explain their findings and,
where appropriate, begin treatment and provide a
personalised plan.

How long is an appointment?

An initial consultation is 40 minutes. Subsequent
appointments may be 20, 30, 40 or 60 minutes
depending on the booking type and your clinical needs.

What should I wear?

Wear comfortable clothing that allows the area to
be assessed. Shorts are useful for lower-limb concerns
and a singlet can help with shoulder or upper-body
assessment. Your comfort, consent and privacy will
always be respected.

What should I bring?

Please bring any referral, claim documents, relevant
scans or reports, your Medicare or private health card,
and any braces or footwear related to your concern.

What if I am running late?

Please call the clinic as soon as possible. To avoid
delaying the next patient, a late arrival may result
in a shorter appointment while the scheduled fee
still applies.

Fees, private health & Medicare

Where can I see your consultation fees?

Our current consultation, Pilates, NDIS and
additional service fees are published on the

Consultation Fees page

Please confirm the correct appointment type when booking.

Can I claim private health insurance?

Yes, eligible physiotherapy services can usually
be claimed through participating private health funds,
and HICAPS is available. Your rebate and out-of-pocket
gap depend on your insurer, policy and annual limits,
so check directly with your fund.

Do you charge a gap fee?

Your gap is the difference between the consultation
fee and any rebate paid by Medicare or your private
health fund. Because rebates vary, we cannot guarantee
a particular gap amount.

Can I receive a Medicare rebate?

If your GP has placed you on an eligible GP Chronic
Condition Management Plan, you may access up to five
allied-health services per calendar year, shared across
eligible providers. You pay the full consultation fee
and Medicare currently rebates

for an eligible physiotherapy service. Eligibility
and allocation are determined by your GP.

Are there weekend or card surcharges?

The published fee schedule lists a $10 Saturday
surcharge, $15 Sunday surcharge and $20 public-holiday
surcharge. It also lists a $0.50 debit-card surcharge
and a 1.1% credit-card surcharge. Please check the
current Fees page before attending.

Is there a Seniors Card discount?

A $10 discount applies to eligible consultations when
a valid Seniors Card is presented. Please tell reception
before payment.

Treatment & recovery

What conditions do your physiotherapists treat?

We assess and treat a broad range of musculoskeletal,
sports, post-operative and rehabilitation concerns,
including back and neck pain, joint problems, muscle
and tendon injuries. Suitability is confirmed through
an individual assessment.

Will I receive treatment at my first visit?

Usually, yes. After assessment and with your consent,
your physiotherapist will begin treatment when it is
clinically appropriate and safe.

What treatments might be used?

Your plan may include education, hands-on techniques,
massage, joint mobilisation, exercise rehabilitation,
dry needling or other modalities. Treatment is selected
for your needs and preferences—not applied as a
standard package.

Is dry needling or manipulation suitable for everyone?

No. Your physiotherapist will screen for risks,
explain potential benefits and side effects, discuss
alternatives and obtain informed consent before using
these techniques.

Will I need scans or imaging?

Not always. Many conditions can be assessed clinically.
If imaging or medical review may be useful, your
physiotherapist will discuss the appropriate next step
and can liaise with your GP.

How many sessions will I need?

This varies with your condition, goals, health and
response to care. Your physiotherapist will recommend
a plan after assessment and review progress with you
rather than promising a fixed number of visits.

NDIS, home care, DVA, TAC & WorkSafe

Do you see NDIS participants?

Yes. We can assist eligible self-managed, plan-managed
and suitable agency-managed participants, subject to
confirmation and a service agreement. The published
physiotherapy rate is $183.99 per hour, with 30 minutes
at $92 and 45 minutes at $138.

What are your Home Care Package rates?

Home Care Package physiotherapy is $220 for 60 minutes
and $350 for 90 minutes. Provider approval, available
funding and any applicable travel, administration or
reporting arrangements should be confirmed before the
appointment.

Do you treat TAC clients?

Yes, subject to claim details and approval. Please
provide your claim number, date of injury and proof of
claim acceptance. Billing follows the current TAC
physiotherapy arrangements; the former Early Intervention
Physiotherapy Framework ended on 1 February 2026.
Reception will confirm whether direct billing or upfront
payment applies to your claim.

Do you treat WorkSafe clients?

Yes, subject to claim acceptance. Please provide your
claim number, date of injury, insurer details and a
current Certificate of Capacity. Reception will confirm
the applicable billing pathway before treatment.

Do you treat DVA clients?

Yes. Eligible Veteran Card holders require a valid
referral. Gold Cards generally cover clinically
necessary treatment for accepted needs, while White
Cards cover accepted conditions. Physiotherapy usually
operates in treatment cycles of up to 12 sessions or
one year, whichever comes first. We can confirm
eligibility and billing before your visit.

Are home visits available?

Home visits may be available depending on your location,
clinical suitability, therapist availability and funding
arrangements. Please contact reception to discuss your
needs.

Clinical Pilates & exercise

What is Clinical Pilates?

Clinical Pilates is an individually tailored exercise
program guided by a physiotherapist or suitably
qualified instructor. It can support strength,
mobility, control and rehabilitation goals.

Do I need an assessment before starting?

An initial assessment is required before private
Clinical Pilates so we can understand your goals,
screen for concerns and select appropriate exercises.
Group participation requirements will be confirmed
when booking.

Can I participate while pregnant?

Many people can exercise safely during pregnancy with
appropriate modifications. Please tell us that you are
pregnant and disclose any complications. Your
physiotherapist may recommend medical clearance
depending on your circumstances.

How large are the Clinical Pilates groups?

Our published Group Clinical Pilates packages are
designed for a maximum of four people per session,
allowing closer supervision and exercise progression.

Can I claim Pilates through private health?

Physiotherapy-led clinical exercise may be claimable
when it meets your fund's requirements, but general
Pilates may not be. Ask your insurer about your specific
policy and item eligibility before booking.

Clinic locations & access

Where are your clinics?

Our Elwood clinic is at Level 1, 61 Brighton Road,
Elwood VIC 3184. Our Beaumaris clinic is at
19 Keys Street, Beaumaris VIC 3193.

Is the Elwood clinic accessible?

The clinic is on the first floor with lift and
wheelchair access. Onsite parking is located between
Medical One and Chemist Warehouse, with public
transport and supporting medical services nearby.

Is the Beaumaris clinic accessible?

Beaumaris has ground-floor, step-free wheelchair
access, with free street parking nearby and a
medical centre next door.

Can I bring a support person or interpreter?

Yes. You are welcome to bring a support person,
subject to your consent and room capacity. If you
need an interpreter or other assistance, please tell
reception in advance so arrangements can be discussed.

Bookings, cancellations & privacy

Please provide at least 48 hours' notice. The
published late cancellation or rescheduling fee
is $50. If you cannot attend in person, ask whether
telehealth is clinically appropriate and available.

How is my health information handled?

Your information is handled confidentially and in
accordance with applicable privacy obligations.
You may request access to records; administration
or report fees may apply.

What should I do in an emergency?

Physio Cure is not an emergency service. For a
medical emergency, call Triple Zero (000) or attend
the nearest emergency department.

Policies in plain English

General informed consent policy

Before care begins, your physiotherapist will explain
the proposed assessment or treatment, expected benefits,
material risks, alternatives and the option of no treatment.
You may ask questions, decline any part or withdraw consent
at any time. Consent is an ongoing conversation and may be
reconfirmed when treatment or circumstances change.

Chaperone and support person policy

You may request a chaperone or support person. We may also
recommend a chaperone for sensitive care. Your preference,
and whether a chaperone was offered, accepted or declined,
may be documented.

Children, young people and decision-making capacity

Consent is obtained from the patient where they have capacity,
or from an authorised parent, guardian or substitute
decision-maker where required. The young person’s understanding,
views, privacy and best interests remain central.

Telehealth and home-visit consent

We will explain the limitations, privacy considerations and
safety arrangements relevant to remote or home-based care.
Telehealth may not be appropriate for every condition.

Dry needling information and consent

Dry needling is optional. Your physiotherapist will explain
the intended purpose, alternatives and risks, which may include
temporary soreness, bruising, bleeding, dizziness, fainting,
infection, nerve or blood-vessel injury and, when treating near
the chest, the rare risk of pneumothorax. Separate consent is
obtained before treatment.

Joint mobilisation and manipulation information and consent

Your physiotherapist will screen your suitability and explain
the proposed technique, expected benefits, alternatives and
material risks. These may include temporary soreness, symptom
aggravation, sprain or strain, and rare serious complications
depending on the body region. You may choose mobilisation,
another treatment or no treatment.

Clinical Pilates and group exercise participation

Exercises are selected according to assessment and may be
modified or stopped. Tell your clinician about pain, dizziness,
breathlessness, pregnancy, recent surgery or any change in
health before participating.

Privacy and health information policy

We collect health and personal information reasonably necessary
to provide care, manage appointments, process payments and
rebates, communicate with authorised parties and meet legal
obligations. Information is used or disclosed for the purpose
collected, or otherwise with consent or where authorised or
required by law.

Confidentiality and permitted disclosure

Clinical information is kept confidential. With consent,
relevant information may be shared with treating practitioners,
referrers, insurers, funding bodies or authorised representatives.
Disclosure may occur without consent only where legally permitted
or required, including certain serious safety risks.

Access, correction and transfer of records

You may request access to or correction of your health
information. We will verify identity and respond under applicable
privacy and health-record laws. Lawful administration charges may

Storage, security, retention and disposal

We use reasonable physical, technical and administrative
safeguards for electronic and paper records. Access is limited
to authorised people. Records are retained and securely destroyed
in accordance with applicable requirements and professional
obligations.

Data breach response

Suspected privacy incidents are contained, assessed and
documented. Where legal notification thresholds are met,
affected people and the relevant regulator will be notified
and remedial action taken.

AI-assisted documentation policy

Approved technology may assist with drafting clinical or
administrative documentation. It does not replace professional
judgement. The treating clinician remains responsible for
reviewing, correcting and approving every clinical record.
Identifiable health information is not entered into public
consumer AI tools.

Optional voice recording consent

We will inform you before any audio is captured and seek specific
consent. You may decline, pause or stop recording without
affecting your care. Any approved system must have appropriate
security and privacy controls. Recordings are not used for
marketing, research, training or model development without
separate consent.

Email, SMS and electronic communication

Electronic communication carries some privacy risk. We use the
contact details you provide for appointments and care-related
communication, apply reasonable safeguards and avoid sending
unnecessary sensitive detail.

Website cookies and analytics

Our website may use essential cookies and limited analytics to
operate and improve the service. The website privacy notice
should identify the tools used, information collected and
available choices.

Cancellation, rescheduling and late arrival

Please provide at least 48 hours’ notice. A $50 late
cancellation or rescheduling fee may apply. Late arrival may
shorten the available treatment time while the scheduled fee
remains payable. Where suitable and available, ask whether
telehealth is possible.

Fees, rebates and payment

Fees and applicable surcharges are displayed on our Fees page.
Private health and Medicare rebates depend on eligibility and
cover. Patients remain responsible for charges not paid by an
insurer, funding body or rebate scheme.

Products, equipment hire, returns and refunds

Purchase and hire conditions should be confirmed before payment.
Hygiene-sensitive or fitted products may have limited return
rights except where consumer guarantees apply. Deposits, hire
periods, damage and late-return charges must be explained at
supply.

Funded-care documentation and billing

Medicare, DVA, NDIS, TAC, WorkSafe and home-care pathways have
different referral, approval and billing requirements. Patients
must provide current and accurate documents, and reception will
confirm the applicable payment pathway.

Respectful behaviour and professional boundaries

Patients, visitors and staff must behave respectfully. Harassment,
discrimination, threats, abuse or unsafe conduct may lead to the
appointment ending or service restrictions, subject to clinical
safety and legal obligations.

Infection prevention and clinical safety

Equipment, treatment areas and hand hygiene are managed using
appropriate infection-control practices. Tell us before attending
if you have a contagious illness or new health concern.

Clinical incidents and emergencies

Adverse events and incidents are documented, assessed and managed
openly. Physio Cure is not an emergency service. Call Triple Zero
(000) for a medical emergency.

Accessibility, interpreters and inclusive care

Please tell us about access, communication, cultural or
interpreter needs when booking. We will make reasonable efforts
to support safe and understandable care.

Dignity, Modesty & Sensitive Care

Your comfort and consent come first

Sensitive-area assessment and treatment

Before touching or exposing a sensitive area, the clinician
will explain the clinical reason, exact area, proposed contact,
clothing adjustment, draping and alternatives. Consent is
specific to the explained assessment or treatment and may be
withdrawn immediately.

Clothing, exposure and draping

Only the area reasonably necessary for assessment or treatment
should be exposed. Privacy, appropriate draping and time to
change will be provided. You may decline any clothing adjustment.

Chaperones and support people

A chaperone will be offered for sensitive care and may be
requested at any time. Whether it was offered, accepted or
declined may be recorded. A support person may attend with your
consent where clinically and practically appropriate.

Physio Cure currently provides external women’s health
assessment and treatment only.

We do not perform internal vaginal or rectal examinations.
The clinician will explain all proposed contact, positioning,
clothing adjustment and draping before proceeding.

Treatment of minors

Age, capacity, maturity, parental or guardian authority,
privacy and best interests are considered. A parent, guardian,
support person or chaperone may be required or recommended
depending on the circumstances.

Photography, observers and withdrawal of consent

Clinical photography, video, students or observers require
separate permission. You may refuse or withdraw permission
without affecting access to appropriate care.

Physiotherapy combines assessment, education, hands-on care and progressive exercise to help people recover from injury, move more comfortably and return to the activities that matter to them.

What is physiotherapy?

Care that helps you understand, move and recover.

Physiotherapists are university-trained, registered health
practitioners with knowledge of the human body, movement
and physical function. A physiotherapist may assess your
movement, strength, joints, muscles, nerves and ability to
complete meaningful activities.

Your care is adapted to your symptoms, health history,
goals, preferences and response to treatment. This may
combine clear explanations, appropriate hands-on care and
a progressive rehabilitation program.

Your treatment plan should make sense to you.

Is this the same as a Functional Capacity Evaluation?

Not necessarily. A pre-employment functional assessment is usually focused on the inherent physical

How long does an assessment take?

Not an injection

Different from acupuncture

What is dry needling?

A needling technique used within physiotherapy.

A trained physiotherapist inserts a fine filiform needle through the skin into selected muscle or soft tissue based on your assessment.

The aim may be to influence pain sensitivity, muscle tone or movement for some patients. Responses vary, and it is not suitable for every patient or every body area.

Important:
Dry needling does not inject medication and should not be presented as a stand-alone cure.

Fine needle placed into selected soft tissue following assessment

Possible benefits

What may improve?

When appropriate, dry needling may help to:

Reduce pain or muscle tightness.

Improve movement, flexibility or muscle function.

Support a return to work, sport and everyday activities.

Results vary between individuals.
No treatment can guarantee a successful outcome.

Safety screening

Tell your physiotherapist before treatment if:

You are pregnant or may be pregnant.

You take anticoagulant or antiplatelet medication, have a bleeding disorder, or bruise or bleed easily.

You have diabetes or epilepsy.

Your immune system is weakened by a condition or medication.

You have an infection, open wound, rash or skin condition near the proposed treatment area.

You have a known metal allergy, particularly nickel or chromium.

You have fainted, felt unwell or experienced panic with needles, injections or blood tests.

You plan to fly or scuba dive within 24–48 hours after proposed needling near the neck, shoulder, upper back or chest.

Safety & informed consent

Understand the possible side effects and risks.

Dry needling is generally considered safe when performed by an appropriately trained physiotherapist, but inserting a needle through the skin involves possible side effects and risks.

Common and usually mild

These usually settle within a few days.

Muscle soreness for 24–72 hours.

Minor bruising.

A small amount of bleeding.

Feeling tired.

A temporary increase in symptoms.

Less common

Dizziness or fainting.

Nausea or sweating.

Skin irritation.

Temporary tingling or numbness from irritation of a nearby nerve.

Rare but serious

Although extremely rare, a collapsed lung (pneumothorax) may occur if dry needling is performed around the neck, shoulder, upper back or chest. This requires immediate medical assessment.

Seek immediate medical care for warning symptoms.

After dry needling around the neck, shoulder, upper back or chest, go to your nearest Emergency Department immediately if you develop difficulty breathing, shortness of breath, chest pain—especially with a deep breath—a persistent cough or blue lips. Tell staff that you recently had dry needling.

Both use fine needles, but the clinical rationale and professional framework may differ.

Questions before dry needling

Clear answers before you decide.

Do I have to have dry needling?

No. Dry needling is optional. You may decline it or withdraw consent at any time without affecting access to other physiotherapy care at Physio Cure.

Does dry needling hurt?

Experiences vary. You may notice a brief pinprick, pressure, a dull ache or a short muscle twitch. You can ask your physiotherapist to pause or stop at any time.

How many needles are used?

There is no fixed number. If dry needling is appropriate, the number and location depend on your assessment, the area being treated and your preferences.

Can I exercise afterwards?

Many people can continue with suitable activity, but advice depends on your presentation and response. Follow the individual guidance provided by your physiotherapist.

Can I have dry needling while pregnant?

Pregnancy must be discussed before treatment. Your physiotherapist will assess whether it is appropriate and explain alternatives where needed.

What if I take blood-thinning medication?

Tell your physiotherapist about all anticoagulant and antiplatelet medication, including medicines such as warfarin, clopidogrel, rivaroxaban, apixaban or aspirin. Your clinician will assess the bleeding and bruising risk before recommending treatment.

We are here to help you understand whether dry needling may be relevant to your physiotherapy plan.

What causes the clicking sound?

A click or pop is generally associated with a pressure

mean that bones have been put back into place, and a

Is mobilisation the same as manipulation?

What do we mean by myofascial release?

Myofascial release describes a range of manual
techniques intended to influence symptoms, movement
and comfort through pressure, positioning and movement
of soft tissues. Different clinicians use the term in
different ways, and the technique is selected because
it is clinically appropriate—not simply because an area
feels “tight”.

Careful wording matters

We do not claim that hands-on treatment “breaks
adhesions”, permanently changes fascia, identifies one
universal pain source or guarantees pain relief.

What is fascia?

Fascia is connective tissue associated with muscles
and other body structures. Pain is complex and cannot
always be traced to one tissue, tender point or
“restriction”.

Systematic reviews in selected musculoskeletal
presentations report variable short-term changes in
pain, movement or function. Findings differ by
condition and comparison treatment, so they do not
support a universal benefit or guaranteed result.

Where used, myofascial techniques are best considered
one possible component of a broader plan. They should
not replace appropriate diagnosis, medical care,
exercise or active rehabilitation when these are
needed.

Jaw pain and temporomandibular disorders

Jaw pain is complex. Your assessment should be clear.

Jaw pain can involve the jaw joints, chewing muscles, teeth, nerves or other medical factors. We assess what physiotherapy may safely help, explain the findings in plain language and refer you when another clinician is better placed to investigate your symptoms.

External assessment first

Consent before every examination or treatment

Dental or medical referral when appropriate

Screen beyond the jaw

We consider dental, medical and neurological
features—not only muscles and joints.

Explain before contact

You are told what is proposed, why it may be useful
and what alternatives exist.

Build active strategies

Education and appropriately dosed exercise are
central to the plan.

Refer when appropriate

We collaborate with your dentist, GP or another
practitioner when needed.

Make a clearer first decision

Could physiotherapy be relevant?

A physiotherapy assessment may be reasonable when jaw
symptoms affect comfortable movement or everyday
function. These signs do not, by themselves, identify
a diagnosis.

Pain with jaw use

Discomfort with chewing, speaking or yawning that is
limiting normal activities.

Movement feels restricted

Reduced comfortable opening, altered movement or
intermittent catching or locking.

Muscle fatigue or tenderness

Symptoms around the temples, cheeks or jaw, including
symptoms associated with clenching.

Jaw and neck symptoms together

Jaw symptoms occurring with relevant neck discomfort
or headache features.

Painless clicking is common.

A jaw click without pain, locking or loss of function
may not require treatment and is not, on its own,
proof of damage. Seek assessment if it worries you
or your symptoms change.

Some jaw symptoms need urgent care

Call Triple Zero (000)

Call 000 for jaw discomfort with chest pain,
pressure or tightness; breathlessness; sweating;
nausea; dizziness; or pain spreading to the arm,
back or neck. Difficulty breathing or swallowing
is also an emergency.

Seek prompt medical or dental assessment

After significant trauma; if the jaw is stuck open
or closed; or if symptoms include facial swelling,
fever, difficulty eating or drinking, vision changes,
a severe unusual headache, new weakness or numbness,
or electric-shock-like facial pain.

Do not try to force a jaw that appears dislocated
back into place.

Understanding the terminology

TMJ is the joint. TMD describes a group of conditions.

Clear language helps prevent a joint sound or tender
muscle from being treated as a complete diagnosis.

TMJ

The anatomical joint

The temporomandibular joint connects the lower jaw
to the skull on each side of the face.

TMD

A group of disorders

Temporomandibular disorders can involve the jaw
joints, chewing muscles and related structures.

Context

Not one simple cause

Symptoms can reflect multiple interacting biological,
behavioural and social factors. A single “root cause”
cannot always be identified.

The right clinician

Who should assess your symptoms?

Jaw-area pain can sometimes arise from teeth, ears,
sinuses, nerves, inflammatory conditions or other
medical problems. Referral is a normal part of
safe care.

Physiotherapist

For movement-related pain, muscle tenderness,
reduced function and relevant neck factors.

Dentist or oral-health clinician

For tooth pain, bite concerns, dental infection,
oral disease, significant wear or appliance advice.

GP, specialist or emergency care

For systemic, neurological, traumatic or unexplained
symptoms, or any warning signs above.

A broad group of techniques

What is manual therapy?

Manual therapy describes skilled, hands-on techniques used by a physiotherapist as part of assessment or
treatment. The aim may be to explore movement, provide short-term symptom relief, improve confidence with
movement or help you participate in exercise and daily activities.

Responses vary. Your physiotherapist should review whether the technique is helping and modify or stop it
when appropriate.

Manual therapy is not a cure or passive quick fix.
It is usually combined with education and
active rehabilitation when appropriate.

Possible options

Techniques we may use

Does manual therapy hurt?

Will I need joint manipulation?

What is therapeutic taping?

Therapeutic taping uses adhesive tape applied to the skin to provide external support, sensory feedback or a
temporary change in how a person experiences movement. Different tapes and application methods have different
purposes.

Taping does not repair tissue by itself and it does not replace diagnosis, progressive exercise, load
management or medical review when these are needed.

Will taping fix my injury?

What is ergonomics?

Can you assess my home office?

Shockwave therapy:
a condition-specific treatment option

Shockwave therapy may be considered for selected persistent tendon-related, plantar heel or other carefully
assessed presentations. Evidence, device type and suitability differ by condition.

Understanding the treatment

What is shockwave therapy?

Shockwave therapy delivers mechanical pressure waves to a selected treatment
area through a hand-held device and coupling gel. Treatment settings are chosen
according to the device, body area, diagnosis, clinical plan and your tolerance.

Radial pressure-wave therapy

Pressure waves disperse from the handpiece and are generally used for more
superficial musculoskeletal targets.

Focused shockwave therapy

Waves converge at a focal point. Focused and radial technologies have
different energy profiles and should not be presented as interchangeable.

Your physiotherapist will explain the available device and whether its use is
appropriate for your presentation. Shockwave is not promoted as breaking down
all scar tissue, regenerating every injury or guaranteeing that surgery can be avoided.

Foot

Plantar heel pain

Selected persistent plantar fasciopathy presentations after other causes are considered.

Achilles

Achilles tendinopathy

Selected mid-portion or insertional presentations, with treatment adapted to the clinical situation.

Outer elbow

Tennis elbow

Persistent lateral elbow tendinopathy where shockwave is a reasonable option after assessment.

Knee

Patellar tendinopathy

Selected persistent presentations, usually alongside progressive loading and activity planning.

Outer hip

Gluteal tendinopathy

Some greater trochanteric pain presentations may be considered following differential assessment.

Shoulder

Calcific rotator-cuff tendinopathy

Only when the diagnosis, device, treatment type and clinical pathway make shockwave appropriate.

Additional selected uses

Frozen shoulder

It may be discussed only as an adjunct within a broader management plan.
Evidence and protocols are heterogeneous.

Golfer's elbow

the evidence is less developed than for lateral elbow tendinopathy.

Bursitis requires a diagnosis

Bursitis is not one uniform condition. Similar symptoms can arise from a
bursa, tendon or another structure, so suitability depends on assessment.

Not routinely promoted for these presentations

Acute muscle strains

Acute ligament sprains

General or non-specific soft-tissue healing

Evidence for routine use in these areas is limited or emerging. Shockwave
should not be described as healing every soft or hard tissue injury.

Delayed or non-union fractures

Confirmed delayed or non-union fracture requires imaging, orthopaedic oversight
and an appropriate specialist focused-shockwave pathway. It is not presented as
routine radial shockwave treatment at Physio Cure.

Proposed mechanisms

Proposed effects include mechanotransduction, cellular signalling and pain
modulation. Tissue and vascular responses continue to be studied, and the
clinical relevance differs across conditions.

Shockwave does not simply break up every adhesion or guarantee tissue regeneration.

Treatment schedule

There is no single schedule for every condition. Frequency and total treatments
depend on the diagnosis, device, dose, irritability, response and rehabilitation plan.

More frequent treatment is not automatically better. Your physiotherapist will
explain the proposed schedule before treatment.

Individual response

Some people improve, some notice little change and some may experience a temporary
increase in symptoms. Progress is reviewed rather than promised to a fixed timeline.

We assess your condition, symptoms, health history, previous care and goals.

Discuss

We explain expected benefits, uncertainty, sensations, risks, fees and alternatives.

With consent, gel and the handpiece are applied and intensity is adjusted to tolerance.

We review your response and progress exercise, loading or activity where appropriate.

How treatment may feel

You may feel repeated tapping, pressure or discomfort. Intensity can be adjusted,
and you can ask to pause or stop at any time.

Temporary soreness, redness, bruising, swelling, altered sensation or increased
symptoms can occur. Tell your physiotherapist if the sensation is unacceptable.

Balanced evidence

Evidence varies by diagnosis, device, dose and study quality. We follow contemporary
literature and professional guidance to inform appropriate use.

We screen for relevant health conditions, pregnancy, bleeding or clotting concerns,
medication, infection, wounds, altered sensation, tumour near the area, recent surgery,
fracture, injection, implants and growth-plate considerations.

This is not a complete screening list. Treatment may be modified, deferred or require
medical input depending on your circumstances.

An adjunct to rehabilitation

Shockwave is generally considered as one possible component of care. Education,
activity planning and progressive exercise may remain important parts of treatment.

Common sciatica questions

Do I need an MRI or X-ray before seeing a physiotherapist?

Not usually. Imaging is not routinely needed at the first
assessment when serious pathology is not suspected. GP or
specialist review may be recommended when imaging could change
management, symptoms are severe or progressive, or warning signs
are present.

Can physiotherapy cure sciatica?

No guaranteed cure can be promised. Physiotherapy may support
pain management, movement, activity and function for some
presentations. The plan depends on the suspected cause, severity,
neurological findings and response; medical or specialist care
may sometimes be required.

Is walking good for sciatica?

Short, tolerable walks can be useful for many people. The
appropriate amount varies. Reduce or modify walking if it causes
a marked or lasting increase in leg symptoms, and seek assessment
if weakness develops.

What if I have numbness, weakness or foot drop?

Numbness and weakness require neurological assessment. New foot
drop or increasing weakness needs prompt medical review. New
bladder or bowel changes or saddle numbness require emergency
assessment.

How long does sciatica take to improve?

Recovery varies according to the cause, severity, duration and
neurological findings. No fixed timeline should be promised.
Progress should be reviewed and care escalated when weakness
increases or recovery is not occurring as expected.

When might a GP, injection or surgical opinion be needed?

Medical or specialist review may be appropriate for severe or
progressive weakness, cauda equina warning signs, significant
unremitting symptoms, concerns about another diagnosis, or
persistent disabling symptoms despite appropriate conservative care.

Do I need a GP referral?

A referral is not usually needed for private physiotherapy.
Your physiotherapist may recommend GP review when medication,
imaging, medical investigation or specialist referral should
be considered.

Evidence for individual physiotherapy interventions in sciatica
is mixed. Recommendations should therefore be individualised,
reviewed against meaningful outcomes and integrated with medical
care when needed.

Do I need an X-ray, CT scan or MRI?

Realistic expectations

How long might recovery take?

There is no single recovery time for all muscle, joint and tendon injuries. Your physiotherapist can
provide a more meaningful estimate after assessment and update it according to your progress.

Muscle strains

Mild injuries may improve within several weeks. Larger or recurrent tears commonly require longer
rehabilitation.

Joint sprains

Recovery may range from weeks to months depending on tissue damage, instability and functional demands.

Tendinopathy

Meaningful capacity change commonly requires consistent progressive loading over at least several weeks
and sometimes months.

Arthritis or persistent pain

Management focuses on function, strength, confidence and symptom control rather than promising a cure.

Symptoms that should not wait for a routine appointment

Seek urgent medical assessment for major trauma, obvious deformity, a suspected fracture or dislocation,
inability to bear weight, a hot swollen joint with fever, sudden calf swelling with chest pain or shortness
of breath, or rapidly progressive weakness or numbness. Call Triple Zero (000) for life-threatening
symptoms.

Do I need a scan before seeing a physiotherapist?

Not always. Your physiotherapist can assess the symptoms and function, then discuss whether medical
review or imaging may be appropriate. Imaging is not required for every muscle, joint or tendon problem.

Should I completely rest a tendon injury?

Complete rest is not usually the long-term solution for tendinopathy. Activity may need to be modified,
followed by a progressive loading program based on symptoms, capacity and goals.

Can physiotherapy help an old or recurring injury?

Physiotherapy may identify ongoing mobility, strength, balance, load-tolerance or movement problems and
provide a graded plan. Assessment is needed to decide whether physiotherapy is appropriate for your
presentation.

Will I receive hands-on treatment?

Hands-on treatment may be offered when clinically appropriate, but it is not automatically required. Your
physiotherapist will discuss the options, obtain consent and usually combine relevant symptom-relief
strategies with active rehabilitation.

What should I bring to my appointment?

Bring relevant referrals, imaging or reports, your claim details if applicable, and clothing that allows
the affected area to be assessed comfortably. You may ask for treatment to be modified or stopped at any
time.
