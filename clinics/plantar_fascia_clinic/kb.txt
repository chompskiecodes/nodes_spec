### The 3-Stage Recovery Framework (Pain-Free Map)

#### Stage 1: Initial Stage (Pain Relief Focus) — Timeline: 2 weeks
Reduce stiffness and inflammation. Offload the plantar fascia to allow healing. Taping techniques. Muscle releasing. New habits and removal of aggravating behaviours.

#### Stage 2: Rehabilitation (Restore Function) — Timeline: Weeks 2 to 6
Improve mobility of feet, ankles, and hips. Strengthen weak and deconditioned muscles. Re-introduce load progressively and monitor. Specific targeted exercises. Pain tracking.

#### Stage 3: Maintenance and Optimisation — Timeline: Week 6 onwards
3 core exercises to maintain results. Transition to barefoot shoes if possible. Ongoing plan to prevent recurrence.

## WHAT IS PLANTAR FASCIITIS

Plantar fasciitis (more accurately plantar fasciopathy) is degeneration or breakdown of the plantar fascia — a strong band of connective tissue running along the bottom of the foot from the heel bone to the base of the toes. It supports the arch, absorbs shock, and contributes to propulsion when walking. The condition typically develops when foot muscles weaken or joints stiffen, forcing the fascia to absorb too much workload.

### Common Symptoms
Sharp pain with first steps in the morning.
Pain after sitting or periods of inactivity — stabbing or tearing sensation when standing again.
Pain throughout the day, especially when standing, walking, or running.
Aching at night.
Tenderness around the heel or sole, usually radiating from the heel along the arch.

### Other Conditions to Rule Out
Not all heel pain is plantar fasciitis. Conditions that can present similarly include: heel spurs, Achilles tendonitis, tarsal tunnel syndrome, stress fractures, bursitis, neuropathy, rheumatoid arthritis, and fat pad atrophy. A thorough assessment helps determine the exact cause.

#### What is The Plantar Fascia Method?

People dealing with heel pain, arch pain, morning foot pain, or recurring plantar fascia symptoms who want a clear step-by-step plan rather than relying only on short-term fixes.

There is both a book and an online course. The book explains the method, principles, phases, and exercises. The online course expands on this with video demonstrations, guided tests, and a structured program to follow at home.

#### What does the online course include?
Video guidance for the key exercises, self-tests, taping techniques, mobility drills, strengthening work, and the full step-by-step recovery process outlined in the book.

#### How is this different from just stretching my foot?
The program does not treat plantar fascia pain as only a foot problem. It looks at the whole movement chain including the foot, ankle, calf, shin, big toe, and hip, to address the underlying reasons the plantar fascia is being overloaded.

#### What does the book cover?
What plantar fascia pain is, why it happens, common mistakes, pain relief strategies, rehabilitation exercises, self-testing, footwear, barefoot shoe transition, maintenance, and what to do if pain returns.

#### What are the main phases of the program?
Three phases: pain relief, rehabilitation, and maintenance. First calm symptoms, then rebuild movement and strength, then maintain results long term.

#### How long does the program take?
Six weeks. Weeks 0 to 2: pain relief. Weeks 2 to 6: rehabilitation. Week 6 onwards: maintenance.

#### Will I need special equipment?
The main essentials are a firm massage ball and rigid strapping tape. Optional extras include toe spreaders, a balance tool, small weights, and minimalist or barefoot shoes.

#### Do I need to be fit to do this program?
No. The program starts with simple pain-relief techniques and progresses into mobility, balance, and strengthening exercises based on individual self-test results.

#### How much time does it take each day?
Around 10 to 20 minutes per day during the pain relief and rehabilitation phases. The maintenance phase takes much less time.

#### What if I have tried orthotics, injections, or other treatments before?
The program is designed for people who have tried short-term solutions without lasting results. It focuses on improving how the body moves so the plantar fascia is no longer constantly overloaded.

#### Does the course show me how to do the exercises properly?
Yes. The online course includes visual demonstrations of exercises, tests, and techniques including ball rolling, low-dye taping, foot and ankle pumps, balance work, hip stretches, big toe mobility, shin strengthening, and calf and ankle mobility.

#### How do I know which exercises I need?
The program uses guided self-tests. Passing a test means no extra work is needed in that area. Failing a test directs the course to the matching corrective exercise.

#### What are the self-tests for?
To identify where the body may be restricted, weak, or poorly coordinated — looking at hip mobility, single-leg balance, hip flexor tightness, big toe extension, shin strength, and calf and ankle mobility.

#### Why does the program include hip exercises?
The hip plays a major role in how the foot functions. When the hip is stiff or poorly controlled, the foot compensates, which may increase stress on the plantar fascia.

#### Does the program include taping?
Yes. The pain relief phase includes low-dye taping to support the plantar fascia while symptoms calm down. Taping is used as a short-term support, not a permanent solution.

#### Does this help with morning heel pain?
Yes. Morning pain is a key focus. The program includes specific strategies such as foot and ankle pumps, ball rolling, and taping to help reduce morning pain and stiffness.

#### Will I have to stop walking or exercising?
Not necessarily. The program teaches how to manage load and avoid repeatedly irritating the fascia. In some cases, temporarily reducing activities that flare symptoms may be needed while rebuilding tolerance.

#### Are barefoot shoes part of the program?
Barefoot or minimalist shoes are discussed as a possible long-term option, but not something to rush into. The program explains when and how to transition safely if appropriate.

#### Do I need barefoot shoes to get results?
No. Barefoot shoes are optional. The core of the program is pain relief, movement restoration, strengthening, and long-term maintenance.

#### What happens after the six weeks?
The maintenance phase includes simple ongoing habits: ball rolling, single-leg balance, and one or two exercises that helped most.

#### What if the pain comes back later?
Return to the pain relief tools early, reduce irritating loads, and then return to maintenance once things settle. The program explains exactly what to do.

No. The book and course are educational resources and do not replace individual medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment. If there is severe pain, unusual symptoms, sudden changes, or a medical concern, seek professional guidance.

Yes. The program is designed for stubborn and recurring plantar fascia pain, focusing on gradually calming symptoms, restoring movement, and rebuilding the body's ability to handle load.
