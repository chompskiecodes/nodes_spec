# Physio Cure

## About Physio Cure

### Overview

Physio Cure provides physiotherapy and rehabilitation from two Bayside Melbourne clinics: Physio Cure in Elwood and Physio First in Beaumaris. The clinic takes time to understand a patient's condition, goals and the activities that matter to them, and the treatment plan may include hands-on physiotherapy, exercise rehabilitation, education and other appropriate techniques based on individual needs.

### The CURE Approach

Care follows four stages. Comprehensive assessment: listening, assessing the condition and understanding goals. Understanding you: the treatment plan is based on the individual's needs. Restore movement: working with the patient to improve movement, strength and function. Empower your recovery: helping the patient understand their condition and take an active role in their own recovery.

### Our History

Physio Cure opened in Elwood in 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic, building trust in the local community through personalised, practical care. In 2021 the practice expanded to a second Bayside clinic, Physio First in Beaumaris, bringing the same patient-centred approach to a second community.

### Our Founder

Physio Cure was founded by Dr Hany "Harry" Bebawy, who graduated at the top of his physiotherapy class from Charles Sturt University with an overall result of almost 90 per cent. His stated aim is to provide thoughtful, evidence-informed care that helps every patient feel understood and supported.

### Mission, Vision and Values

The mission is to provide thoughtful, evidence-informed physiotherapy that reduces barriers, supports recovery and helps people return to the activities that matter to them. The vision is to be a trusted Bayside physiotherapy team known for personalised care, clinical integrity and meaningful long-term outcomes. The stated values are: Listen First — understand the person, not only the symptoms; Act With Integrity — offer clear, honest and clinically appropriate advice; Work Together — make patients active partners in their recovery; Keep Improving — learn, review and adapt care; Welcome Everyone — provide respectful and inclusive support.

### Awards and Recognition

Physio Cure was named Best Physiotherapist in the City of Glen Eira, VIC, at the Quality Business Awards in both 2024 and 2025. This award was given to the practice's former Glen Eira clinic.

### Clinical Spaces

Both clinics offer private treatment rooms for assessment, hands-on treatment and individual care, a rehabilitation area with strength, balance and mobility equipment, and a Clinical Pilates studio with reformer-based and supervised exercise options.

An initial consultation is 40 minutes; subsequent appointments may be 20, 30, 40 or 60 minutes depending on the booking type and clinical needs. Wear comfortable clothing that allows the relevant area to be assessed — shorts are useful for lower-limb concerns and a singlet can help with shoulder or upper-body assessment. Comfort, consent and privacy are always respected.

#### What should I bring to my appointment?

Bring any referral, claim documents, relevant scans or reports, a Medicare or private health card, and any braces or footwear related to the concern.

## Services

#### Physiotherapy Assessment and Treatment

Physiotherapists are university-trained, registered health practitioners who assess movement, strength, joints, muscles, nerves and the ability to complete meaningful activities. The first appointment typically involves discussing symptoms, health history and goals, assessing relevant movement and function, explaining findings, and — where appropriate and with consent — beginning treatment during the same visit. Ongoing care may combine education, hands-on techniques, exercise rehabilitation and progressive activity, reviewed and adjusted over time rather than following a fixed number of sessions. No referral is generally required for a private appointment; a referral or supporting documents may be required for Medicare, DVA, TAC, WorkCover and some other funded pathways.

#### Do I need a referral for a private physiotherapy appointment?

No referral is normally required. A referral or other documentation may be required for Medicare GP Care Plans, DVA, WorkCover, TAC or some other funded pathways.

#### Will treatment begin at my first appointment?

Often yes. The first appointment starts with an assessment, and treatment may begin during the same consultation when it is safe and appropriate, after the physiotherapist explains the options and obtains consent.

#### Which treatment approach is right for me?

This depends on assessment findings, health history, goals, preferences and the available evidence. The physiotherapist explains which options may be suitable and why, rather than applying one standard package to everyone.

#### Do I need scans or imaging first?

Not always. Many conditions can be assessed clinically; where imaging or medical review may be useful, the physiotherapist discusses the appropriate next step and can liaise with the patient's GP.

#### Manual Therapy

Manual therapy describes skilled hands-on techniques a physiotherapist may use during assessment or treatment, including graded joint movements, soft-tissue techniques and guided movement assistance, chosen after assessment and used to support — not replace — active rehabilitation. High-velocity thrust manipulation is a distinct technique requiring its own specific assessment, explanation and informed consent, and is never automatic. Possible temporary responses include soreness, discomfort, bruising, dizziness or a short-lived increase in symptoms; the physiotherapist uses the least forceful reasonable approach and monitors comfort throughout, and treatment can be modified or stopped at any time. Alternatives to manual therapy include exercise and graded activity, load or task modification, or medical review or other referral when indicated.

#### Does manual therapy hurt?

It should not require the patient to tolerate unwanted pain. Some techniques can feel firm or create temporary soreness; the physiotherapist should be told how the patient feels so the approach can be changed or stopped.

#### Will I need joint manipulation?

No. Manipulation is not automatically required — other manual techniques, exercise, education or no hands-on treatment may be more appropriate, and any manipulation requires separate assessment and informed consent.

#### Joint Mobilisation and Manipulation

Mobilisation is a controlled, low-velocity repeated or sustained movement applied to a joint, graded to the patient's comfort and adjustable or stoppable at any time. Manipulation is a quick, precise, controlled high-velocity low-amplitude thrust used only after assessment when appropriate, may produce a click or pop (a normal pressure-related joint phenomenon, not a sign that "a bone has gone back into place"), and requires a specific discussion and informed consent — it is not required for treatment to be useful. Before treatment, the physiotherapist screens for factors such as osteoporosis or low bone density, previous spinal/pelvic/rib fracture or spinal surgery, recent significant trauma, inflammatory arthritis or ligament instability, suspected bone or spinal infection, unexplained fever/night pain/weight loss, known bone cancer, blood-thinning medication or a bleeding disorder, neurological changes (increasing weakness, numbness, tingling, saddle numbness, or bladder/bowel changes), a history of stroke/TIA/arterial dissection/aneurysm or sudden severe headache/neck pain, fainting or severe dizziness, and pregnancy. Possible effects include temporary soreness, stiffness or headache (commonly settling within 24–48 hours), and — rarely — cervical manipulation carries a very rare risk of cervical artery dissection, stroke, or spinal cord/nerve-root injury; other regions carry very rare risks of worsening disc injury, nerve damage, fracture or joint injury.

#### What causes the clicking sound during manipulation?

A click or pop is generally associated with a pressure change and release of gas within the joint. It does not mean bones have been put back into place, and a click is not required for treatment to be useful.

#### Is mobilisation the same as manipulation?

No. Mobilisation uses controlled, low-velocity repeated or sustained movement. Manipulation uses a quick, controlled low-amplitude thrust and requires a separate suitability and consent discussion.

#### What if I feel unwell after manipulation?

Contact the clinic for unexpected or concerning symptoms. Call 000 immediately for sudden facial droop, arm or leg weakness, speech difficulty, severe new dizziness or unsteadiness, double or lost vision, difficulty swallowing, or an unusual severe headache. Seek urgent emergency assessment for inability to pass urine, loss of bladder or bowel control, saddle numbness around the buttocks/genital area/thighs, or progressive leg weakness.

#### Dry Needling

Dry needling involves a trained physiotherapist inserting a fine, sterile, single-use filiform needle through the skin into selected muscle or soft tissue, based on assessment, aiming to influence pain sensitivity, muscle tone or movement. It is not an injection (no medication or fluid is delivered) and is different from acupuncture in its clinical rationale. Before treatment the patient is screened for pregnancy, anticoagulant/antiplatelet medication (such as warfarin, clopidogrel, rivaroxaban, apixaban or aspirin) or a bleeding disorder, diabetes or epilepsy, a weakened immune system, infection/open wound/rash/skin condition near the area, a known metal allergy (particularly nickel or chromium), a history of fainting or feeling unwell with needles, and any plan to fly or scuba dive within 24–48 hours of needling near the neck, shoulder, upper back or chest. Common, usually mild effects include muscle soreness for 24–72 hours, minor bruising, a small amount of bleeding, tiredness or a temporary increase in symptoms; less common effects include dizziness or fainting, nausea or sweating, skin irritation, or temporary tingling/numbness from irritation of a nearby nerve. A rare but serious risk is a collapsed lung (pneumothorax) if needling is performed around the neck, shoulder, upper back or chest — patients are told to go to the nearest Emergency Department immediately if they develop difficulty breathing, shortness of breath, chest pain (especially with a deep breath), a persistent cough or blue lips after such treatment, and to tell staff they recently had dry needling.

#### Do I have to have dry needling?

No. It is optional, and can be declined or withdrawn at any time without affecting access to other physiotherapy care at Physio Cure.

#### Does dry needling hurt?

Experiences vary — a brief pinprick, pressure, a dull ache or a short muscle twitch may be felt, and the patient can ask the physiotherapist to pause or stop at any time.

#### How many needles are used?

There is no fixed number; if dry needling is appropriate, the number and location depend on assessment, the treated area and the patient's preferences.

#### Can I have dry needling while pregnant?

Pregnancy must be discussed before treatment. The physiotherapist assesses whether it is appropriate and explains alternatives where needed.

#### Myofascial Release

Myofascial release describes a range of manual techniques using pressure, positioning and movement of soft tissue, selected because it is clinically appropriate rather than simply because an area feels "tight." Physio Cure does not claim that hands-on treatment "breaks adhesions," permanently changes fascia, identifies one universal pain source, or guarantees pain relief; systematic reviews (in a 2021 chronic low-back pain review and a 2024 chronic neck pain review, evidence summary reviewed August 2026) report variable short-term changes in pain, movement or function that differ by condition and comparison treatment. It is best considered one possible component of a broader plan alongside education, activity guidance, mobility work, progressive strengthening or medical review, and should not replace appropriate diagnosis, medical care, exercise or active rehabilitation when these are needed. Contraindication screening covers recent surgery, unhealed wounds, skin infection, recent fracture, blood-thinning medicine or a bleeding disorder, osteoporosis, cancer, pregnancy, vascular conditions, altered sensation or unexplained symptoms; treatment is not applied over an unhealed surgical wound. Urgent medical advice is needed for new unexplained one-sided leg swelling, pain, warmth or redness; call 000 for chest pain, sudden shortness of breath, coughing blood or collapse.

#### Is myofascial release the same as massage?

The terms and techniques can overlap, but "myofascial release" usually refers to selected sustained or moving pressures used within a clinical plan; the physiotherapist explains the intended technique rather than relying on the label alone.

#### Can myofascial release replace exercise?

No. Hands-on care does not replace active rehabilitation when movement, strength, conditioning or gradual return to activity is indicated — it may be used alongside those strategies.

#### Remedial and Sports Massage

Provided in a clinical physiotherapy setting (not a spa massage), remedial massage begins with the physiotherapist reviewing symptoms, health history, goals and preferred pressure, and screening suitability. Techniques include gentle-to-deeper pressure adapted to the patient, trigger-point techniques for localised symptoms, myofascial techniques, and sports massage adapted to training and recovery goals, combined where useful with movement and exercise advice. The patient may ask to change pressure, reposition, pause or stop at any time; only the agreed treatment area is exposed with professional draping. Massage may need modification or postponement for a suspected blood clot or deep-vein thrombosis, acute fracture or severe new injury, fever/infection/burns/open wounds or infectious skin changes, unexplained swelling or neurological symptoms, blood-thinning medicine or a bleeding disorder, osteoporosis or fragile skin, recent surgery, cancer treatment, pregnancy or a complex health condition.

#### How firm will the massage pressure be?

Pressure is agreed with the patient and adapted throughout the session; firmer pressure is not automatically better and treatment does not need to be intensely painful to be useful.

#### Is soreness normal after massage?

Some people experience temporary tenderness, soreness or tiredness afterward. Tell the clinic if symptoms are severe, worsening or concerning, and seek medical help for urgent symptoms.

#### Can massage fix the cause of my pain?

No single treatment can be assumed to fix every cause of pain. Massage may support short-term comfort or movement for some people; assessment helps determine whether education, exercise, load modification, medical review or other care should also be considered.

#### Exercise Rehabilitation Programs

An individually tailored exercise program is built from a clinical assessment of relevant movement, strength, endurance, balance, coordination and functional tasks, then progressed as capacity grows. Programs specify the exercise, dosage (sets, repetitions, distance, holds or time), intensity, frequency and planned progression, and may combine supervised in-clinic rehabilitation with an independent home or gym program using minimal equipment where possible. Patients are told to continue as agreed when the response is expected and manageable, to modify and check in with the physiotherapist if symptoms are stronger or last longer than expected, and to stop and seek help for new severe pain, major swelling, new weakness or numbness, fainting, chest pain or serious breathing difficulty (call 000 for the latter).

#### Do I need gym equipment for my exercise program?

Not necessarily. Programs can often use body weight, household items or simple equipment; if equipment would be useful, the physiotherapist can discuss practical options.

#### Is some discomfort during exercise acceptable?

The acceptable response varies by presentation. The physiotherapist explains what is expected, what to monitor and when to modify or stop; severe, unusual or worsening symptoms require review.

#### Clinical Pilates

Clinical Pilates is an individually tailored exercise program guided by a physiotherapist or suitably qualified instructor, supporting strength, mobility, control and rehabilitation goals including post-operative recovery, sports rehabilitation, strength and balance, and postnatal return to exercise. An initial assessment is required before private Clinical Pilates; previous Pilates experience is not required. Group Clinical Pilates sessions are capped at a maximum of four participants for closer supervision. Pregnant patients should disclose the pregnancy and any complications, as medical clearance may be recommended depending on circumstances. Physiotherapy-led clinical exercise may be claimable through private health extras cover depending on the fund's rules; general Pilates or fitness classes may not be — confirm with the fund before booking.

#### Is Clinical Pilates suitable for my condition?

Suitability depends on the individual presentation, goals and assessment; the physiotherapist determines whether Clinical Pilates is appropriate as part of the rehabilitation plan.

#### What should I wear to a Clinical Pilates session?

Comfortable clothing that allows free movement, so the physiotherapist can observe technique and the patient can comfortably perform the exercises.

#### Group Classes (Clinical and General Pilates)

Physio Cure offers two supervised small-group pathways, each running 50-minute sessions. Group Clinical Pilates is a clinically guided option requiring an initial physiotherapy assessment, suited to selected post-operative, musculoskeletal or neurological rehabilitation goals, with individual exercise plans delivered within a shared session; one-to-one treatment may still be required alongside it. Supervised General Pilates is a broader supervised group option for participants who can safely follow the class format without the same degree of individual clinical rehabilitation, requiring assessment or pre-exercise screening beforehand. Both require the participant to tell the supervisor before exercising about new or worsening symptoms, recent surgery, falls, dizziness, fainting, chest symptoms, pregnancy or any important medical change; call 000 for an emergency such as chest pain, severe breathing difficulty or signs of stroke. Wear comfortable clothing and enclosed, supportive footwear unless advised otherwise, and bring water and any necessary medication such as a prescribed reliever inhaler.

#### Do I need an assessment before joining a group class?

Yes. Group Clinical Pilates requires an initial physiotherapy assessment, and Supervised General Pilates also requires appropriate screening or assessment beforehand.

#### Can exercises be changed for me within a group class?

Exercises may be adjusted within the safe scope and format of the class; if needs cannot be appropriately managed in the group, one-to-one assessment or treatment may be recommended instead.

#### Is there a private health rebate for group classes?

Possibly, depending on the service, treating provider, item claimed, the patient's policy and fund rules — a rebate is not guaranteed, so contact the fund before attending if reimbursement affects the decision.

#### Can I attend a group class after surgery?

Only when the treating clinician considers group exercise appropriate and any required medical or surgical instructions have been obtained; timing and restrictions vary, so no standard return date can be promised.

#### Cupping Therapy

Cupping is offered as one possible modality to support muscle relaxation and more comfortable movement, generally used alongside — not instead of — the broader assessment-led treatment plan. As with other adjuncts, suitability is confirmed by the treating physiotherapist and informed consent is obtained before use.

#### Shockwave Therapy

Shockwave therapy delivers mechanical pressure waves to a treatment area through a hand-held device and coupling gel; radial pressure-wave therapy disperses from the handpiece for more superficial targets, while focused shockwave converges at a focal point — the two are not interchangeable. It may be considered for selected persistent presentations including plantar heel pain (plantar fasciopathy), Achilles tendinopathy, lateral elbow tendinopathy (tennis elbow), patellar tendinopathy, gluteal tendinopathy/greater trochanteric pain, calcific rotator-cuff tendinopathy, and — as an adjunct only — frozen shoulder or golfer's elbow. It is not routinely promoted for acute muscle strains, acute ligament sprains, general soft-tissue healing, or delayed/non-union fractures (which require imaging and orthopaedic oversight). There is no single treatment schedule; frequency depends on diagnosis, device, dose, irritability and response, and more frequent treatment is not automatically better. Patients may feel repeated tapping, pressure or discomfort, with intensity adjustable and the option to pause or stop at any time; temporary soreness, redness, bruising, swelling, altered sensation or increased symptoms can occur. Screening covers pregnancy, bleeding/clotting concerns, relevant medication, infection, wounds, altered sensation, tumour near the area, recent surgery, fracture, injection, implants and growth-plate considerations. The shockwave add-on fee is charged in addition to the physiotherapy consultation fee (see Fees and Funding).

#### How many shockwave treatments will I need?

There is no universal number; any proposed schedule depends on the condition, device, clinical reasoning and response. More treatment is not automatically better.

#### Is shockwave therapy painful?

It can feel uncomfortable; intensity can be adjusted, and the patient can ask to pause or stop at any time.

#### Is shockwave therapy covered by private health insurance?

Cover varies between insurers and policies — check directly with the fund. The shockwave add-on fee is separate from the physiotherapy consultation fee.

#### Can shockwave be combined with exercise?

Often yes, when clinically appropriate. Progressive exercise, load management and activity planning may be important parts of care for tendon-related presentations.

#### Taping

Therapeutic taping uses adhesive tape applied to the skin to provide external support, sensory feedback or a temporary change in movement experience; it does not repair tissue by itself and does not replace diagnosis, progressive exercise, load management or medical review. It may be trialled for selected ankle or knee presentations for short-term support during specific tasks, or where a patient finds it changes comfort or awareness. Before taping, the physiotherapist asks about adhesive allergies, fragile skin, wounds, altered sensation, circulation problems or previous tape reactions. Patients are told to remove the tape promptly if they develop increasing pain, burning, itching, marked redness, rash, swelling, numbness, tingling or colour change, and to seek urgent medical care for severe symptoms, breathing difficulty or signs of a serious allergic or circulation problem. The taping fee is $15 when clinically indicated (see Fees and Funding).

#### Will taping fix my injury?

No. Taping may provide temporary support or feedback for some people, but it does not heal an injury by itself; a broader management plan may be recommended.

#### Can I shower or exercise with tape on?

Some tapes are water resistant — pat rather than rub dry, and follow the specific instructions given at the appointment. Exercising while taped is only appropriate if the activity itself suits the injury and plan; tape should not be used to push through unsafe symptoms.

#### Can anyone be taped?

No. Skin integrity, allergy history, sensation, circulation and other health factors need to be considered first.

#### How should tape be removed?

Follow the physiotherapist's advice — generally, peel slowly in the direction of hair growth while supporting the skin; warm water or oil may help.

#### Ergonomics and Posture Assessment

An ergonomic assessment considers workstation setup (chair, desk, screen, keyboard, mouse, tools), task demands (workload, repetition, force, reach, time pressure), movement variation, and how symptoms respond during and after tasks. Physio Cure does not present one "correct" posture; the aim is a reasonably comfortable setup combined with regular movement, task variation and graded capacity for the work required. Assessments can be conducted in-clinic (using photos, measurements or video) or as a workplace visit by prior arrangement. Outputs may include an assessment summary, prioritised practical changes, a movement plan, and a written report on request (additional fees may apply). For employers, the assessment is one input only and does not replace the employer's legal occupational health and safety responsibilities.

#### Can you assess my home office?

Yes, depending on availability and location; a clinic consultation using clear photos, measurements or video may also help, though it cannot fully replace an on-site review.

#### Do I need to buy new equipment after an ergonomic assessment?

Not necessarily — the clinic usually starts with practical adjustments using what is already available, and discusses any equipment options and limitations without guaranteeing a result.

#### Can you provide a written ergonomic report?

A report can be discussed before the appointment — tell the clinic who needs it and why; report-writing fees and consent for sharing information may apply.

#### Does an ergonomic assessment replace an employer's OHS assessment?

No. It may provide clinical or ergonomic recommendations, but it does not transfer or replace the employer's legal workplace-safety responsibilities.

#### CAM Walker Boot Fitting

A CAM walker (moon) boot may protect or restrict movement for selected lower-limb injuries. The appointment includes assessment or referral review (checking the diagnosis, symptoms, swelling, skin and circulation), sizing and adjustment of the boot for fit and pressure, and walking and device guidance, including prescribed weight-bearing status and any walking-aid needs. Wear instructions (including whether the boot can be removed for sleep, hygiene or exercise) must follow the patient's specific diagnosis and treatment plan rather than a generic schedule; the boot should be kept dry unless otherwise instructed, and should not be altered, padded or have the weight-bearing status changed without advice. Patients are told to check regularly for skin rubbing, blisters or pressure areas, toe colour/warmth/swelling and boot condition, and to contact the treating team promptly for worsening pain, persistent numbness or tingling, marked swelling, blue or pale toes, unusual coldness, skin breakdown or a poorly fitting boot — urgent medical care is needed for possible blood clot signs (new calf pain, swelling, warmth or redness) or an emergency (call 000 for chest pain, sudden shortness of breath, coughing blood or collapse). Patients should not drive if the boot or injury prevents safe vehicle control; a right-sided boot commonly prevents safe driving. Moon boot purchase or hire deposit is $150, with a separate weekly hire fee (see Fees and Funding); consultation or fitting fees are additional.

#### How long will I need to wear the boot?

The timeframe depends on the injury, healing, medical plan and review findings — the clinic cannot safely provide one standard duration or guarantee a healing time.

#### Will I need crutches with a CAM walker boot?

Possibly, depending on the prescribed weight-bearing status, balance, pain and walking safety; if crutches are supplied, they are fitted and explained at the appointment.

#### Can I take the CAM walker boot off to sleep or shower?

Only according to the instructions given for the specific injury — some people must keep the boot on more consistently than others, and it should be kept dry unless specifically designed for water exposure.

#### Can I drive while wearing a CAM walker boot?

Do not drive unless able to safely and legally control the vehicle, with any relevant clinical, licensing and insurance considerations clarified first; a right-sided boot commonly prevents safe driving.

#### What if the CAM walker boot feels too tight?

Check for swelling and pressure symptoms and follow the adjustment advice provided; seek prompt advice for ongoing tightness, numbness, colour change, increasing pain or circulation concerns.

#### Post-Operative Rehabilitation

A staged physiotherapy plan is guided by the patient's procedure, surgeon's instructions, current capacity and goals; physiotherapy does not override the surgeon's restrictions, and if instructions are unclear, conflicting or unavailable, treatment may be modified while clarification is sought. Patients are asked to bring the operation date and exact procedure, discharge summary/referral/operative information, the surgeon's rehabilitation protocol, any weight-bearing/brace/movement restrictions, relevant imaging or reports, a current medicines list, and any brace, crutches, walking aid or orthosis (or to email documents before the visit). The plan generally progresses through protecting and understanding restrictions, restoring essential movement, progressing strength/balance/endurance, reintroducing function, and — where appropriate — preparing for higher-demand recreational or sporting tasks; timing and milestones follow the surgeon's protocol rather than a fixed recovery time. Rehabilitation covers hip/knee replacement, knee ligament or arthroscopic procedures, shoulder procedures, tendon/ligament/soft-tissue repair, spinal procedures, and fracture fixation and other orthopaedic surgery. Seek urgent medical care for chest pain, sudden difficulty breathing, coughing blood or collapse; seek urgent medical advice for new unexplained calf/thigh pain, swelling, warmth or redness (possible blood clot); and contact the surgical team or doctor for spreading redness, increasing warmth/swelling, wound opening, discharge, bad smell, fever or unexpectedly worsening pain.

#### When should physiotherapy start after surgery?

Timing depends on the operation and the surgeon's instructions — some plans begin with early mobility or education, others require protection before particular movements; contact the clinic with discharge information if unsure.

#### Will post-operative rehabilitation be painful?

It does not rely on a "no pain, no gain" approach. Some temporary discomfort may occur with movement or exercise, but the response is monitored and the plan adjusted; severe, unexpected or worsening symptoms require review.

#### How long is the first post-operative appointment and are home visits available?

A standard initial physiotherapy consultation is normally 40 minutes, with the specific appointment type and fees confirmed on the current Fees page when booking. Home visits may be available depending on location, clinical suitability and appointment availability — contact reception to discuss access needs and current fees before booking.

#### Neurological Rehabilitation

Individual physiotherapy is offered to medically stable adults living with an established neurological condition — including stroke, Parkinson's disease and other movement disorders, multiple sclerosis, acquired brain injury, and other stable neurological or peripheral neuromuscular presentations — with care that is assessment-led, goal-focused and coordinated with the patient's healthcare team when needed. Contact the clinic before booking if the patient requires a hoist, two-person physical assistance, continuous medical monitoring, specialised equipment or substantial environmental modifications, so suitability can be confirmed. Assessment covers diagnosis, medical history and medicines, falls/fatigue/pain/symptom behaviour, transfers/walking/balance, strength/coordination, and home/work/community priorities and support needs. Treatment areas may include walking and transfers, balance and falls-risk, strength and endurance, upper-limb movement, mobility and stiffness, and home practice and carer education; no component is automatically appropriate for every condition. With consent, physiotherapy may communicate with the patient's GP, neurologist, rehabilitation physician, occupational therapist, speech pathologist, nurse, orthotist, exercise professional, support coordinator, family or carers; this does not imply every profession is available in-house. For stroke-related care, Physio Cure follows current Australian Stroke Foundation guidance alongside the patient's medical and rehabilitation instructions. New sudden neurological symptoms — face drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty — require an immediate call to 000, even if symptoms improve or disappear; do not wait for a routine physiotherapy appointment.

#### Can a support person attend a neurological rehabilitation appointment?

Yes. Patients may bring a family member, carer, advocate or support person; consent, privacy and preferences remain central throughout.

#### Is the clinic suitable for wheelchair users?

The Elwood clinic is on the first floor with lift access; the Beaumaris clinic has ground-floor, step-free access. Contact reception before booking at either clinic to discuss wheelchair dimensions, transfer needs, bathroom access, parking and any required assistance.

#### How often will neurological rehabilitation appointments be needed?

There is no standard frequency — recommendations depend on assessment, goals, fatigue, risk, response, other services involved and the patient's ability to practise safely between visits.

#### What if I become very tired during neurological rehabilitation exercise?

Exercise and activity are adjusted according to symptoms, fatigue, medical status and response; tell the physiotherapist if feeling unusually tired, unwell, dizzy or unsafe so the session can be reviewed.

#### Sports Injury Physiotherapy

Care covers a new injury (pain, swelling, reduced movement or difficulty using a limb after sport) and persistent or recurring problems that limit training, work or daily life, including muscle strains and tears (hamstring, calf, quadriceps, groin and others), tendon pain and tendinopathy (Achilles, patellar, rotator-cuff, gluteal, elbow), ligament and joint injuries (ankle sprains and selected knee/shoulder/joint injuries), knee and lower-limb pain, shoulder/elbow/upper-limb problems, and running and overuse problems. The first appointment covers the injury history and goals, training load and sport demands, and examination of movement, strength, balance and functional or sport-specific tasks where safe; imaging or medical referral is discussed only where indicated, and most patients can begin without a scan. Rehabilitation generally progresses through protecting and settling the injury, rebuilding capacity, reintroducing sport demands, testing readiness (symptoms, range of movement, strength, balance, speed/power, sport-specific exposure and confidence) and a monitored return, with return-to-sport decisions based on criteria rather than the calendar alone. Seek urgent medical care rather than a routine physiotherapy appointment for a visibly deformed limb, an open wound with visible bone, loss of circulation, severe pain or inability to bear weight, rapidly increasing swelling or bruising, numbness or marked weakness, or breathing difficulty/confusion/loss of consciousness after a head injury (call 000). A suspected concussion requires stopping sport immediately and seeking prompt medical assessment; the patient should not return to sport while symptomatic or until appropriately cleared — physiotherapy cannot clear a suspected concussion.

#### Do I need an X-ray, ultrasound or MRI for a sports injury?

Not necessarily. Many sports injuries can be assessed clinically first; the physiotherapist can discuss whether imaging or medical review appears appropriate.

#### Should I keep training through pain?

This depends on the injury, symptoms and activity — there is no universal pain rule. An assessment helps determine what to stop, modify or safely continue.

#### How long will recovery from a sports injury take?

Recovery varies with the injured tissue, severity, general health, sport demands, consistency and response to rehabilitation. A working prognosis is discussed after assessment and updated as the patient progresses.

#### Can physiotherapy clear a suspected concussion?

No. A suspected concussion requires prompt medical assessment and a staged return process; sport must stop immediately, the patient must not return while symptomatic, and the advice of the appropriately qualified treating team must be followed.

#### Pre-Employment Medical and Functional Assessments

Physiotherapist-led pre-employment functional assessments are matched to the inherent physical requirements of a specific role, agreed with the employer in advance and limited to job-related physical demands. Possible assessment components include a health and injury questionnaire, blood pressure and observations, movement and joint screening, strength and grip testing, balance and coordination, lifting/carrying, push/pull tolerance, reaching and postural tolerance, squat/kneel/step tasks, functional endurance, and role-specific task simulation; the exact tests, appointment length, fee and reporting timeframe are confirmed with the employer before booking. The process is: the employer defines the role and its inherent requirements, the scope (tests, reporting, length, fee, timing) is confirmed, the candidate is informed and gives consent, the physiotherapist completes the agreed functional tasks, and the authorised recipient receives a report addressing the agreed referral question (capacity against stated demands, relevant limitations or precautions, and recommendations or further review) without unrelated health information. Candidates should bring photo identification, the employer's referral or instructions, comfortable clothing, enclosed footwear suitable for physical tasks, any glasses/hearing aids normally used, and requested PPE; medical information is brought only when specifically requested. Employment decisions remain the employer's responsibility under applicable workplace, privacy and anti-discrimination law; the assessment is one input and does not replace the employer's work-health-and-safety risk-management duties, nor is it necessarily equivalent to a comprehensive Functional Capacity Evaluation.

#### How long does a pre-employment assessment take?

Duration depends on the number and complexity of the agreed tests; the appointment length, fee and report timeframe are confirmed after reviewing the role requirements.

#### Who receives the pre-employment assessment report?

The intended recipient is identified before testing, and the candidate is informed about the purpose of the assessment and how relevant information will be shared as part of the consent process.

#### Can a pre-employment assessment be adjusted for accessibility?

Candidates should contact the clinic before the appointment to discuss accessibility requirements, communication needs or reasonable adjustments; any change must still allow the agreed referral question to be addressed appropriately.

#### Is this the same as a Functional Capacity Evaluation?

Not necessarily. A pre-employment functional assessment is usually focused on the inherent physical demands of a proposed role, while a comprehensive Functional Capacity Evaluation may have a broader purpose and scope; the required service is confirmed before booking.

## Conditions We Treat

#### Back Pain

Assessment-led care for new, persistent or recurring back pain, including symptoms that may travel into the buttock or leg. The physiotherapist takes a history and assesses movement, strength, daily function and relevant neurological signs, then explains findings and agrees a practical plan; treatment may include activity and load planning, graded movement, work and daily-life strategies, and selected manual therapy or adjuncts only when clinically appropriate, always alongside — not instead of — active rehabilitation. Imaging (X-ray or MRI) is not routinely required for uncomplicated low back pain and is considered only when findings suggest a serious condition or are likely to change management; prolonged bed rest is not generally recommended. Most private patients do not need a referral, though one may be required for certain Medicare, DVA, WorkCover or TAC pathways. Call 000 for a medical emergency (chest pain, breathing difficulty, fainting), and seek urgent medical assessment for new loss of bladder or bowel control or numbness around the saddle area, sudden or worsening leg weakness or widespread numbness, back pain after major trauma or possible fracture, fever with feeling very unwell, or severe unremitting pain with concerning night pain, unexplained weight loss or a history of cancer.

#### Should I rest or keep moving with back pain?

Prolonged bed rest is generally not recommended for uncomplicated low back pain — the physiotherapist helps identify tolerable activities and gradually build movement.

#### Can physiotherapy help back pain that travels into my leg?

Physiotherapy may be appropriate for some people with back-related buttock or leg symptoms; assessment is needed to screen neurological signs and determine whether physiotherapy, medical review or referral is appropriate.

#### Neck Pain

Assessment-led care for new, persistent or recurring neck pain, including symptoms that may travel into the shoulder, arm or hand. Assessment covers neck and shoulder movement, strength, function and relevant nerve signs, screening for anything needing medical review before treatment. Care may include comfortable movement and graded neck/shoulder exercise, practical advice for workstations/driving/lifting/sleep/sport, and mobilisation or soft-tissue techniques where appropriate (manipulation requires separate suitability screening and consent); imaging is not needed for most people with neck pain. Seek urgent care (call 000 for possible stroke, traumatic injury or chest symptoms) for neck pain after a traumatic accident or major fall, new or worsening weakness/numbness/loss of coordination, difficulty walking or new balance problems, new bowel or bladder changes, fever/sweats/chills with neck pain, sudden severe headache or neck pain with confusion/seizure/double vision/severe loss of balance, or new facial droop/slurred speech/swallowing difficulty/chest pain/shortness of breath.

#### Is there one correct posture for neck pain?

No single posture suits everyone. Comfortable variety, regular movement breaks, a suitable workstation setup and gradually building capacity may be more useful than holding one rigid position.

#### What if pain or tingling travels into my arm or hand from my neck?

Arm or hand symptoms can have different causes; assessment may include strength, reflex, sensation and nerve testing to determine whether physiotherapy, medical review, imaging or referral is appropriate.

#### Headache and Migraine

Physiotherapy may be useful when headache occurs alongside neck pain, stiffness, restricted movement or other musculoskeletal factors; migraine and new or unusual headaches require assessment and management by a GP or specialist, and physiotherapy is not presented as a cure for migraine or a substitute for medical diagnosis. Neck-related headache features include headache with neck pain or stiffness, reduced or painful neck movement, symptoms affected by a sustained neck position, and pain beginning at the back of the head. Tension-type headache tends to feel pressing or tightening, often on both sides, and may coexist with neck and shoulder tension. Migraine-type features are pulsing or throbbing pain, nausea or vomiting, and sensitivity to light, sound or movement, needing GP-led diagnosis and medical care. Assessment covers headache history/pattern/triggers, screening for warning signs, and examination of the neck and related musculoskeletal contributors. Treatment may combine education and pacing, tailored neck/upper-back exercise, graded general activity, manual therapy or mobilisation for neck-related headache, and dry needling only as an optional adjunct with informed consent — not as a migraine cure. Progress is tracked against headache days per month, intensity/duration, neck movement/function, sleep/work/activity participation and triggers, with a headache diary useful for discussion with the prescriber. Call 000 or seek urgent care for a sudden severe or "worst-ever" headache, new weakness/numbness, speech or vision change, loss of balance, seizure, collapse, confusion, fever with a stiff neck or rash, or a severe headache after head injury; prompt medical review is also needed for a new or progressively changing headache during pregnancy/postpartum, after age 50, with cancer or reduced immunity, or one triggered by coughing or exertion.

#### Can physiotherapy cure migraine?

No. Physiotherapy is not presented as a cure for migraine — it may help address coexisting neck pain, movement limitations or physical activity factors, while migraine diagnosis and medical management remain with a GP or specialist.

#### Can I attend a physiotherapy appointment during an acute migraine attack?

Contact the clinic first — severe, new or unusual symptoms may need medical assessment. If the presentation is a familiar, medically diagnosed migraine without warning signs, rescheduling or an assessment can be discussed.

#### Clinical evidence behind headache and migraine care

Evidence supports cautious use of exercise and some manual approaches for appropriately assessed cervicogenic headache, but certainty varies and migraine requires appropriate medical diagnosis and management. Sources referenced include NICE guidance on headaches in adults, the International Classification of Headache Disorders (migraine), and two 2022 and 2024 reviews of manual and exercise therapy for cervicogenic headache.

#### Sciatica

Sciatica describes nerve-related symptoms — pain travelling from the back into the buttock and leg, with possible pins and needles, numbness or weakness — and is a symptom pattern rather than one structural diagnosis; possible contributors include disc herniation, foraminal narrowing, spinal stenosis or spondylolisthesis, and other conditions (hip/sacroiliac, muscle/tendon, peripheral nerve or vascular problems) can mimic it. Assessment includes the full symptom history, screening for urgent/serious causes, neurological testing (strength, reflexes, sensation) and movement/function assessment; imaging is not usually needed at the first assessment unless serious pathology is suspected. Care may include education and pacing, continuing safe movement (modifying rather than stopping activity), tailored exercise, gentle neural mobility work where appropriate, and — as adjuncts only, not a substitute for active care or referral — soft-tissue work or dry needling; shockwave, ultrasound and (per NICE guidance) TENS are not offered as routine core treatment for sciatica. While waiting for an appointment, general low-risk steps include changing position regularly rather than staying in bed, short comfortable walks if tolerated, modifying tasks that sharply increase leg symptoms, and heat or cold if comfortable and skin sensation is normal. Seek emergency assessment (attend an emergency department or call 000) for new loss of bladder or bowel control, saddle-area numbness, severe or rapidly worsening weakness, or symptoms in both legs with bladder/bowel change; new foot drop or increasing leg weakness also needs prompt medical assessment.

#### Do I need an MRI or X-ray before seeing a physiotherapist for sciatica?

Not usually. Imaging is not routinely needed at the first assessment when serious pathology is not suspected; GP or specialist review may be recommended when imaging could change management or warning signs are present.

#### Is walking good for sciatica?

Short, tolerable walks can be useful for many people. Reduce or modify walking if it causes a marked or lasting increase in leg symptoms, and seek assessment if weakness develops.

#### When might a GP, injection or surgical opinion be needed for sciatica?

Medical or specialist review may be appropriate for severe or progressive weakness, cauda equina warning signs, significant unremitting symptoms, concerns about another diagnosis, or persistent disabling symptoms despite appropriate conservative care.

#### Clinical evidence behind sciatica care

Evidence for individual physiotherapy interventions in sciatica is mixed, so recommendations are individualised and reviewed against meaningful outcomes alongside medical care when needed. Sources referenced include NICE guidance on low back pain and sciatica, Healthdirect Australia on spinal disc problems and cauda equina, and 2023 reviews on physiotherapy interventions for sciatica and on rehabilitation for low back pain with or without radiculopathy.

#### Whiplash

Whiplash describes a movement mechanism (rapid acceleration/deceleration affecting the neck's muscles, joints, ligaments and nervous system) rather than one identical injury, and its symptoms can overlap with concussion or more serious injury — the clinic screens first before starting rehabilitation. A scan may be normal even when symptoms are real; imaging is usually reserved for findings suggesting fracture, spinal cord injury or another serious condition, and there is no "putting the neck back into place." Guideline-aligned care centres on active rehabilitation: clear education, staying active with modification rather than prolonged rest, gentle neck movement, low-load isometric and progressive strengthening, and manual therapy only as an adjunct alongside active care; prolonged rest, routine soft collars, and dependence on passive treatment are not routine first-line care, and needling/taping/manipulation have limited evidence in acute whiplash. Many people improve within weeks, though some take months, and progress is tracked with measures such as the Neck Disability Index, neck movement, headache/dizziness/sleep impact, and driving/work/activity tolerance. Physio Cure can assess privately and work with TAC pathways where applicable — bring the TAC claim number, injury date and relevant correspondence. Do not drive with pain or stiffness that prevents safe head turning, or with dizziness, visual disturbance, confusion, reduced concentration or medication-related drowsiness.

#### How soon should I start physiotherapy after whiplash?

Medical assessment comes first, especially when symptoms are severe or warning signs are present. Once serious injury is excluded, early advice, gentle movement and an individual active plan may support recovery.

#### Should I wear a neck collar or rest after whiplash?

Prolonged rest and routine soft collars are generally not recommended; usual activity may be modified and gentle movement introduced as tolerated unless a medical practitioner has advised otherwise.

#### Can Physio Cure see whiplash patients with a TAC claim?

Yes. Physio Cure can provide physiotherapy for eligible TAC patients — contact reception before booking and bring the claim number, date of injury and relevant medical or insurer correspondence, since funding and approval depend on the individual claim.

#### Clinical evidence behind whiplash care

This guidance is based on Australian public-health and compensable-injury sources, including Healthdirect Australia (whiplash and concussion), SIRA's acute whiplash guidelines and quick reference guide, and TAC whiplash recovery resources.

#### Shoulder Pain

Shoulder pain can arise from the shoulder itself, the neck, nerves, or occasionally another medical condition; assessment screens for urgent concerns and explains whether physiotherapy, GP review or imaging is the appropriate next step. Assessment covers collision or injury history and any care already received, a fracture/spinal-cord/nerve/concussion warning-sign screen, neck movement and neurological/upper-quarter examination, and impact on headache, dizziness, sleep, driving and work, with baseline pain and disability measures for future review. After a collision, call 000 or go to emergency for loss of consciousness, increasing drowsiness or confusion, seizure, repeated vomiting, severe or worsening headache, new limb weakness or numbness, trouble walking or balancing, loss of bladder or bowel control, or chest pain/breathing difficulty; seek prompt medical review for dizziness, vision or hearing change, difficulty swallowing, or concentration problems.

#### Jaw Pain and TMD

Jaw pain can involve the jaw joints, chewing muscles, teeth, nerves or other medical factors, so assessment considers dental and medical causes, not only muscles and joints, and refers on when another clinician is better placed to help. TMJ refers to the anatomical joint (the temporomandibular joint connecting the lower jaw to the skull); TMD (temporomandibular disorders) describes a group of conditions that can involve the jaw joints, chewing muscles and related structures, without one simple root cause. A physiotherapy assessment may be relevant for pain with jaw use, restricted or altered movement, muscle fatigue/tenderness, or jaw symptoms occurring with neck discomfort or headache; painless clicking alone is common and may not require treatment. Assessment begins externally — any intra-oral (inside the mouth) examination is not automatic and requires a separate explanation and explicit consent, with appropriate gloves and infection-control procedures. Treatment options include education and reassurance, tailored jaw exercise, therapist-assisted mobilisation, muscle-focused techniques, relevant neck and postural exercise, and self-management strategies (pacing, sleep or clenching awareness); ultrasound and shockwave are not presented as routine TMD treatments. A 2023 clinical practice guideline for adults with chronic TMD pain (three months or longer) supports education, supervised jaw exercise, therapist-assisted mobilisation, manual trigger-point therapy and selected behavioural approaches. Call 000 for jaw discomfort with chest pain/pressure/tightness, breathlessness, sweating, nausea, dizziness, or pain spreading to the arm/back/neck, or for difficulty breathing or swallowing. Seek prompt medical or dental assessment after significant trauma, if the jaw is stuck open or closed, or for facial swelling, fever, difficulty eating or drinking, vision changes, a severe unusual headache, new weakness/numbness, or electric-shock-like facial pain; do not try to force a dislocated-looking jaw back into place.

#### Do I need a dentist or a physiotherapist for jaw pain?

It depends on the symptoms. Movement-related pain, muscle tenderness and reduced jaw function may be appropriate for physiotherapy assessment; tooth pain, infection, oral disease, bite or appliance concerns require a dental clinician.

#### Is jaw clicking a sign of damage?

Not necessarily — painless clicking can occur without important loss of function and may not need treatment. Seek advice if it becomes painful, the jaw locks, function changes or the patient is concerned.

#### Shoulder, Elbow, Wrist and Hand Pain

The shoulder, elbow, wrist and hand work as one movement chain, so a diagnosis is not based on the painful spot alone — assessment considers onset, aggravating factors, work/sport demands, movement, strength, sensation and the neck (since nerves from the neck travel into the arm and hand). Shoulder presentations include frozen shoulder (adhesive capsulitis), biceps tendinopathy, AC joint sprain, instability or dislocation rehabilitation after medical clearance, and osteoarthritis or post-injury/postoperative stiffness. Elbow presentations include lateral elbow tendinopathy (tennis elbow), medial elbow tendinopathy (golfer's elbow), UCL and sports-related sprains, olecranon bursitis, elbow osteoarthritis, post-fracture rehabilitation, and ulnar nerve irritation (cubital tunnel). Wrist presentations include sprains and ligament injuries, De Quervain tenosynovitis, TFCC-related ulnar-sided pain, carpal tunnel syndrome, extensor/flexor tendon problems, ganglion-related symptoms, and distal radius or scaphoid fracture rehabilitation. Hand and thumb presentations include thumb-base (CMC joint) osteoarthritis, joint sprains, trigger finger, tendon irritation, grip weakness, finger stiffness after fracture/surgery, and nerve-related symptoms (median, ulnar or radial). Treatment follows a structured assessment and may include education and load management, mobility/isometric/progressive-resistance exercise, selected joint mobilisation or soft-tissue techniques, taping/braces/splints for short-term support, dry needling or shockwave for selected persistent tendon or calcific presentations, and comfort-focused modalities that do not replace active rehabilitation. Seek urgent medical care after major trauma, visible deformity, suspected fracture or dislocation, a cold/pale/blue hand, rapidly increasing swelling, a hot red joint with fever, sudden marked weakness, or worsening numbness and loss of hand function; call 000 for chest pressure, shortness of breath, sweating, nausea or arm pain that may be heart-related.

#### Can a neck problem cause arm or hand symptoms?

Yes. Nerves from the neck travel into the arm and hand, so neck or nerve problems can contribute to pain, pins and needles, numbness or weakness; the neck and nervous system may be screened when relevant.

#### Will I need a brace or splint for an upper-limb injury?

Some wrist, thumb, hand or elbow presentations may benefit from short-term support, selected and fitted for a clear purpose alongside a plan to maintain or rebuild movement and capacity.

#### Muscle, Joint and Tendon Injuries by Region

Shoulder and upper arm: rotator cuff-related pain and tendinopathy, partial-thickness rotator cuff tears, calcific rotator cuff tendinopathy, subacromial pain and bursitis, frozen shoulder and post-injury stiffness, AC joint sprains and biceps tendinopathy. Elbow, wrist and hand: lateral and medial elbow tendinopathy, distal biceps and triceps tendon problems, wrist and finger joint sprains or stiffness, forearm muscle strains, and De Quervain's-related thumb-side wrist pain. Hip, pelvis and groin: gluteal tendinopathy and greater trochanteric pain, hip osteoarthritis and joint stiffness, adductor or groin muscle strains, hip-flexor and quadriceps strains, proximal hamstring tendinopathy, and postoperative or post-immobilisation weakness. Knee and thigh: patellar and quadriceps tendinopathy, patellofemoral pain, meniscal injuries, ACL/PCL/MCL/LCL sprains or rehabilitation, knee osteoarthritis and stiffness, and hamstring or quadriceps strains and contusions. Ankle, calf and foot: lateral ankle ligament sprains and instability, high ankle (syndesmosis) sprains, calf muscle strains, plantar heel pain (plantar fasciopathy), and peroneal and posterior tibial tendon problems. Spine, ribs and jaw: mechanical lower back and neck pain, muscle strains and movement-related stiffness, thoracic spine and rib-joint pain, cervicogenic headache, and deconditioning after injury or inactivity (see the Back Pain, Neck Pain and Jaw Pain sections above for full detail). Suspected fractures, dislocations, complete tendon ruptures, significant trauma and some postoperative presentations may require medical or surgical review before or alongside physiotherapy. Recovery timeframes are not fixed: mild muscle strains may improve within several weeks while larger or recurrent tears take longer; joint sprains may take weeks to months; tendinopathy commonly requires consistent progressive loading over at least several weeks and sometimes months; arthritis and persistent pain are managed for function, strength and symptom control rather than a promised cure. Seek urgent medical assessment for major trauma, obvious deformity, a suspected fracture or dislocation, inability to bear weight, a hot swollen joint with fever, sudden calf swelling with chest pain or shortness of breath, or rapidly progressive weakness or numbness; call 000 for life-threatening symptoms.

#### Do I need a scan before seeing a physiotherapist for a muscle, joint or tendon injury?

Not always — the physiotherapist can assess the symptoms and function, then discuss whether medical review or imaging may be appropriate; imaging is not required for every muscle, joint or tendon problem.

#### Should I completely rest a tendon injury?

Complete rest is not usually the long-term solution for tendinopathy. Activity may need to be modified, followed by a progressive loading program based on symptoms, capacity and goals.

#### Can physiotherapy help an old or recurring injury?

Physiotherapy may identify ongoing mobility, strength, balance, load-tolerance or movement problems and provide a graded plan; assessment is needed to decide whether physiotherapy is appropriate for the presentation.
